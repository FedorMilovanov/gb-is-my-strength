## Purpose

Prepare the second Origins Golden Chapter without retroactively collapsing the South Shtunda into an already-formed Baptist denomination. Every strong formulation must distinguish contemporary self-description, later Baptist identity, missionary reporting, Orthodox polemics, and state evidence.

## Editorial guardrails

- READY claims may be stated directly when the cited source is strong enough for the formulation.
- VERIFY claims require an exact page/locator or a second corroborating source before publication wording is strengthened.
- HOLD claims must not be presented as established fact.
- “Shtunda” is a historical movement/environment and must not be treated as a synonym for “Baptist” without qualification.
- R. Rashkovsky/Ryaboshapka/Ratushny and other southern actors must not be flattened into a single conversion story.
- German/Mennonite/Pietist/Molokan influence must be separated from direct Baptist institutional transmission.
- Later memories cannot silently upgrade the contemporary evidence of the 1860s–1880s.
- Missionary Magazine reports are external contemporary evidence, not neutral omniscient narration.
- Orthodox polemics are evidence of how opponents described the movement; polemical characterization is not automatically a factual description.

## Four-voice architecture

### 1. Local/participant voice

Use local actors where available. Their testimony shows motives, practice and self-description, but each source remains tied to its date and genre.

### 2. Missionary voice

Use missionary reports as contemporary external observation. Preserve who is reporting, why, and what the report could realistically know.

### 3. Opposing/polemical voice

Use Orthodox polemical material to reconstruct perception, conflict and vocabulary. Never silently convert polemical adjectives into neutral facts.

### 4. State/administrative voice

Use administrative measures, inspections, prohibitions and legal decisions where available. Separate a written order from later recollection of its effect.

2. What “Shtunda” meant in context.

4. Contact with Baptist and missionary networks.
5. Baptism and community formation.

9. Bridge to the organizational events of 1884–1885.

## Explicit HOLD list for next content pass

- any numerical claim about the scale of the southern movement without a primary/counting basis;
- any claim that foreign missionaries directly caused a specific local conversion without case-level evidence;
- any attempt to make the southern story a single straight line to the 1944 union.
