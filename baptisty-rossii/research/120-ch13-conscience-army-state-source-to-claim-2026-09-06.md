## 1. Central thesis

Safe synthesis:

> The Soviet state did not abolish conscientious objection in one clean step. It first recognized and judicially administered it, then narrowed who could plausibly claim it, subjected sincerity and denominational history to proof, and simultaneously pressured Baptist and Evangelical Christian institutions to declare armed military service compatible with Christian duty. The church conflict therefore cannot be reduced either to state coercion alone or to a purely internal theological change: law, surveillance, institutional survival, abuse concerns and genuine competing readings of Christian obedience all acted together.

Forbidden simplifications:
- `the 1919 right was abolished in 1923`;
- `all Baptists were pacifists before 1926`;
- `the 1926 decision was a free theological vote with no state pressure`;
- `the 1926 decision was only an OGPU fabrication and had no Baptist doctrinal precedent`;
- `the 1925 law preserved the broad 1919 exemption unchanged`;
- `every young man joining an evangelical church sought draft avoidance`;
- `1929 belongs inside this chapter as its main legal event`.

## 5. XXV Baptist congress, 30 Nov–8 Dec 1923

Strong documentary reconstruction:
- 5 Dec Anti-Religious Commission instructed Tuchkov that if the congress adopted a negative military resolution, measures should be taken to split the congress and try to join the pro-service faction with Evangelical Christians;
- 11 strongly pacifist delegates were arrested during/around the congress in the better-supported documentary line; a competing `21` figure exists in later literature and must not be silently merged;
- named exiles in documentary synthesis include M. D. Timoshenko, I. N. Shilov, N. A. Levindanto, R. D. Khomyak, Glagolev and Printsev;
- the congress nevertheless did **not** adopt a simple compulsory-armed-service formula.

Safe resolution synthesis:
> state obligations were acknowledged; bloodshed was treated as unacceptable; because delegates did not reach one view on how military duty should be fulfilled, the method was left to each believer's personal conscience.

This is materially different from the 1926 resolution.

## 7. 1925 law — exemption survives, but the gate becomes much narrower

USSR Law `Об обязательной военной службе`, 18 Sep 1925; section XVIII.

### Article 216
Potential exemption is limited to citizens who by birth and upbringing belong to families of sects whose teaching prohibits armed military service **at present and prohibited it before 1917**.

### Article 217
Provincial/equivalent courts may invite experts and obtain competent opinions.

### Article 218
Court exemption decision must be obtained/presented within the statutory pre-conscription window.

### Article 219
Without the required court decision, a claimant serves on ordinary terms.

### Article 220
Those exempted and medically fit are assigned to epidemic control or other socially useful work; wartime service can include special rear/front-support teams. Later amendments adjusted implementation.

### Meaning

The right is not gone; eligibility has changed from a broad conscience-centered mechanism toward a historically/denominationally conditioned legal test.

Safe synthesis:
> `1925 сохранил юридический институт освобождения, но сделал его значительно менее доступным для новых или internally divided evangelical pacifists.`

Status: **PRIMARY LAW CLOSED**.

## 8. Why Baptist/Evangelical cases became difficult under Article 216

The legal criterion `teaching prohibited armed service before 1917` matters because Russian Baptist confessional history contains a pro-military-service Article 13 in the 1905/1906 confession tradition.

Therefore the state could distinguish between:
- historically pacifist groups (e.g. some Mennonite/Doukhobor traditions);
- Baptist/Evangelical individuals or factions whose personal pacifism emerged despite a denominational text that could be read as permitting/obliging military service.

Do not state that this automatically barred every Baptist by law without case-level evidence. Courts still adjudicated claims.

Status: **LEGAL-DOCTRINAL INTERACTION STRONG / CASE LAW SAMPLE P0**.

## 9. 1926 — institutional decision changes

### Baptist XXVI Congress
Moscow, 14–18 Dec 1926.

Later official Baptist history reports:
- more than 300 delegates; 239 decisive votes;
- military/state question central;
- P. V. Ivanov-Klyshnikov and Ya. Ya. Vins associated with the pro-service line in research tradition;
- resolution returned to Article 13 of the 1905/1906 Baptist confession;
- **221 voted for, 9 against**.

The 1945 official denominational publication reproduces the resolution as recognizing military service when required by government.

Important dual truth:
1. the formula had genuine pre-Soviet Baptist confessional precedent;
2. the 1926 congress operated after several years of targeted state pressure and arrests of pacifist leaders.

Do not erase either side.

### Evangelical Christian X Congress
The 1945 official denominational witness summarizes its resolution as recognizing military service as a general civic duty (`оброк`).

Status: **RESOLUTION CONTENT STRONG / ORIGINAL 1926 PROTOCOL FACSIMILES P0**.
