## 1. Central thesis

Safe synthesis:

> 1917 opened a public field that Russian Baptists and Evangelical Christians tried to occupy immediately: meetings, congresses, religious-liberty programs, mission, print, youth work and even experiments in political participation. The Bolshevik victory did not erase that field overnight, but changed its legal grammar. By 1918–1921 religious equality, private religious practice and legally recognized conscientious-objection procedures coexisted with loss of corporate property/legal-person rights, tighter administrative supervision, punitive outcomes and episodes of arbitrary local repression. Missionary expansion and coercive state power therefore grew side by side rather than in a simple sequence of “freedom, then persecution.”

Forbidden simplifications:
- `February 1917 instantly solved the religious question`;
- `all Baptists entered politics`;
- `Bolsheviks immediately banned Baptist churches`;
- `the 1918 separation decree was simply a religious-freedom law`;
- `the 1918 decree immediately outlawed private religious teaching`;
- `conscientious objection was freely guaranteed without state examination`;
- `1920 merger created one permanent Baptist/Evangelical union`;
- `the 1921 Tver arrests prove a single uniform central policy already existed`.

## 9. Separation decree — equality and dispossession in one legal text

The decree:
- separates church and state;
- prohibits local measures limiting freedom of conscience;
- permits citizens to profess any religion or none;
- protects rites subject to public order;
- permits private religious learning;
- **denies religious societies property ownership and legal-person rights**;
- nationalizes religious property, with worship buildings potentially granted for use by state decision.

Safe interpretation:
> It dismantled formal confessional privilege and announced individual religious equality while simultaneously removing corporate property/legal-person status and making institutional use of property dependent on state arrangements.

Date control is non-trivial: signed/adopted and official-publication dates differ. File 108 governs display.

## 11. Conscience and military duty — primary legal chain 1918→1919→1920→1921

### 11.1 1918 baseline

Article 6 says religion does not automatically excuse civil obligations, but individual substitutions may be allowed by a people's court.

### 11.2 4 Jan 1919

Primary decree creates a specific judicial procedure:
- sanitary or other socially useful work may replace military service;
- People's Court must obtain Moscow OSROG expertise in each case;
- expertise covers doctrine and individual sincerity;
- exceptional complete exemption can be petitioned to VTsIK.

Safe synthesis: **legal recognition + evidentiary state scrutiny**.

### 11.3 14 Dec 1920

Primary amendment:
- keeps substitution and exceptional full exemption;
- uses witness evidence and expert opinions;
- courts may invite trusted representatives of the relevant teaching, preferably local;
- timely application receives a court certificate;
- timely applicants are not to be detained before the decision.

Closed structural claim:
> mandatory OSROG expert monopoly in this procedure disappears in favor of a more localized court-controlled mechanism.

Do not claim OSROG itself was abolished without a separate institutional source.

### 11.4 16 Sep 1921 retrospective review

VTsIK ordered review of **all** cases of persons convicted for military evasion/desertion on religious grounds, including cases decided by:
- courts;
- anti-desertion commissions;
- Extraordinary Commissions (Cheka).

If sincerity was established under the 1920 evidentiary standard, the review body could release a person from military duty where the cohort was demobilized, or replace imprisonment with permitted socially useful/agricultural/specialty work.

Document: **16 Sep 1921**. Publication: `Известия ВЦИК` №208, **18 Sep 1921**.

Analytical value:
> The state still recognized religious conscientious objection, yet the very need for a general review shows that punitive outcomes had accumulated across several coercive/judicial bodies.

Status: **PRIMARY LEGAL CHAIN CLOSED 1918→1919→1920→1921**. See file 109.
