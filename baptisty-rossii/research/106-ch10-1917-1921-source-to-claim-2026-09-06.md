## 1. Central thesis

Safe synthesis:

> 1917 opened a public field that Russian Baptists and Evangelical Christians tried to occupy immediately: meetings, congresses, religious-liberty programs, mission, print, youth work and even experiments in political participation. The Bolshevik victory did not erase that field overnight, but changed its legal grammar. By 1918–1921 religious equality, private religious practice and even conscientious-objection procedures coexisted with loss of corporate property/legal-person rights, tighter administrative supervision and episodes of arbitrary local repression. Missionary expansion and coercive state power therefore grew side by side rather than in a simple sequence of “freedom, then persecution.”

Forbidden simplifications:
- `February 1917 instantly solved the religious question`;
- `all Baptists entered politics`;
- `Bolsheviks immediately banned Baptist churches`;
- `the 1918 separation decree was simply a religious-freedom law`;
- `the 1918 decree immediately outlawed private religious teaching`;
- `conscientious objection was freely guaranteed without state examination`;
- `1920 merger created one permanent Baptist/Evangelical union`;
- `the 1921 Tver arrests prove a single uniform central policy already existed`.

## 9. January 1918 separation decree — freedom and dispossession in one text

Primary text, Sobr. Uzak. no.18 art.263:

The decree states:
- church separated from state;
- local laws limiting freedom of conscience prohibited;
- citizens may profess any religion or none;
- free religious rites protected subject to public order;
- religion may be taught privately;
- religious societies treated under general rules for private societies;
- **religious societies may not own property and do not have rights of a legal person**;
- religious property declared national property, with worship buildings potentially granted for free use by state decision.

### Chapter interpretation

This text must be presented as structurally double-sided:
- it dismantled formal confessional privilege and announced individual religious equality;
- it simultaneously removed corporate property/legal-person status from religious organizations and made use of worship property dependent on state arrangements.

Do not label it simply `freedom decree` or simply `ban decree`.

Date complexity: adoption/publication dates differ, see file 108.

## 11. Conscience and military duty: accommodation becomes a state-administered procedure

### 11.1 Separation decree baseline

Art.6 of the Jan 1918 separation decree says religion cannot generally excuse civil obligations, but substitutions may be allowed in individual cases by a people's court.

### 11.2 Decree of 4 Jan 1919

Primary decree:
- persons unable by religious conviction to perform military service may, by court decision, substitute sanitary service or other socially useful work;
- People's Court must obtain expertise from the Moscow **Unified Council of Religious Communities and Groups (OSROG)** in each case;
- OSROG expertise covers doctrine and sincerity;
- by unanimous decision OSROG may petition VTsIK for complete exemption in exceptional cases.

This is neither unrestricted exemption nor pure repression: it creates a legally recognized, evidentiary state procedure.

### 11.3 Amendment of 14 Dec 1920

Primary decree:
- retains substitution right;
- court now uses testimony and expert opinions;
- trusted representatives of the relevant religious teaching, preferably local, may serve as experts;
- petitions must be filed at least a week before call-up;
- timely applicants receive a court certificate and must not be detained before decision;
- court can grant complete exemption exceptionally.

Key structural change:
- **mandatory OSROG monopoly is removed/replaced by a more localized court-expertise scheme**.

This thread is developed in file 109 and later belongs centrally to Chapter 13.

Status: **PRIMARY LEGAL CHAIN CLOSED 1918→1919→1920**.

## 14. 1921 Tver youth congress — central/local power divergence

### Event

MASTER rows 624 / 1726 / 2730:
- 6th All-Russian Christian Youth Congress in Tver began 2 May 1921;
- central authorization was reportedly obtained;
- on 5 May local GubCheka arrested **42 participants**;
- later releases followed.

### Archival publication 2024

Krapivin & Metelkin published documents on exactly this case in `Новейшая история России`, 2024, vol.14 no.1, pp.232–259.

The publication confirms:
- arrest of **42** delegates/participants;
- controversy around Tver GubCheka official D. S. Vinogradov;
- appeals to Lenin/Kalinin and diplomatic channels;
- **27 July (other evidence: 28 July) 1921 VTsIK Presidium decision** for conditional early release of imprisoned delegates.

This materially upgrades the event from late confessional memory to archival-document-supported history.

### Production interpretation

This event is ideal to show that early Soviet enforcement was not yet a single frictionless vertical:
- permission could exist at one level;
- local coercion could override it;
- central authorities could later reverse or discipline the result.

Do not use this complexity to deny the reality of repression. The arrest itself is documented.
