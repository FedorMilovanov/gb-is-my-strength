# 122. Chapter 13 — Circular No.237 and the 1925 military-service law — legal control

Дата прохода: 2026-09-06  
Authority: `docs/BAPTISTY-ROSSII-BOOK-AUTHORITY-V2.md`  
Статус: **1923 OPERATIVE TEXT RECOVERED / 1925 PRIMARY LAW CLOSED / FACSIMILE RECEIPTS PARTIAL**

## 0. Question controlled here

Did the Soviet state abolish religious conscientious objection in 1923?

**No.** The evidence now supports a more precise sequence:

> 1919 created a judicial mechanism for religious conscientious objection; 1920 revised the procedure; 1923 Circular No.237 sharply narrowed and evidentially hardened access to that mechanism; the 1925 USSR military-service law preserved a statutory exemption mechanism but expressly conditioned it on birth/upbringing in families belonging to sects whose teaching prohibited armed service both then and before 1917.

Therefore the old shorthand `1923 = abolition` is **REJECTED**.

## 2. Operative rules recovered from the 1923 circular

The full operative rule-set is now recoverable in text even though controlled page-image receipt remains open.

### Rule 1 — historical membership must be evidenced

For members of religious sects historically formed under the imperial regime whose generally binding doctrine required refusal of military service, the applicant had to submit evidence of belonging to that sect together with the claim.

The circular expressly pointed to evidence such as:
- family lists;
- judicial/administrative judgments;
- records of conviction/punishment for refusal of military service by the applicant, parents or relatives;
- similar historical proof.

Meaning:
> personal assertion of conscience was no longer enough; the court was directed toward documentary proof of inherited/historical confessional status.

Status: **TEXT RECOVERED**.

### Rule 2 — enumerated historical sects + narrow analogue clause

The circular identified historical groups whose doctrine counted as requiring refusal of military duty. Documentary scholarship and the reproduced legal text identify the core list as:
- Doukhobors;
- Mennonites;
- Molokans;
- `нетовцы` / related runner-wanderer tradition.

A note allowed persons of other sects (the reproduced legal compilation gives `малеванцы` as an example) to be treated similarly **only if the applicant or the applicant's family had suffered under the imperial regime for refusal of military service**.

This is the key reason A. I. Savin characterizes the rule as making exemption effectively inaccessible to many young post-1917 evangelical pacifists.

Editorial language:
- SAFE: `Circular No.237 imposed a historical/confessional eligibility test and a family-persecution evidence gate.`
- UNSAFE: `Only four sects were absolutely and forever permitted exemption in every case.`

Status: **CORE TEXT/MEANING RECOVERED; EXACT FACSIMILE PUNCTUATION OPEN**.

### Rule 3 — adverse sincerity evidence could defeat the claim without further expert delay

Where the above factual material was absent and there were data casting doubt on the applicant's sincerity/religiosity, the provincial court could deny the exemption request without postponing the matter for an expert examination or other supplementary proof.

Meaning:
> the procedure became not merely denominational but an evidentiary sincerity screen.

Do not convert this into a claim that every court had to reject every Baptist or Evangelical Christian.

Status: **TEXT RECOVERED**.

### Rule 4 — the judgment had to specify the substitute work

When granting relief, courts were directed to comply with the Civil Procedure Code and the 14 Dec 1920 decree. They were not to stop at a bare declaration of exemption from combatant service; the order had to specify replacement by hospital or corresponding work.

Meaning:
> the 1923 circular still presupposed a functioning exemption/substitution procedure. This is direct evidence against the `abolition in 1923` shorthand.

Status: **TEXT RECOVERED / HIGH ANALYTICAL VALUE**.

### Rule 5 — expedited hearing

Exemption cases were to be scheduled outside the general queue for the nearest court session.

Meaning:
> again, the circular regulates adjudication; it does not simply announce that such adjudication no longer exists.

Status: **TEXT RECOVERED**.

## 3. What changed in 1923

The correct legal characterization is **narrowing + proof burden + sincerity control**, not one-step repeal.

Before the mature 1923 restriction, the normative vocabulary centered on a claimant whose religious convictions made military service impossible. Circular No.237 redirected attention toward:
- whether the sect had a pre-revolutionary doctrinal history of refusal;
- whether the applicant could prove historical/family belonging;
- whether family or personal persecution for refusal existed where the sect was not on the core list;
- whether the applicant's sincerity was credible;
- what substitute work a favorable court judgment assigned.

This is why `dynastic principle` is a useful analytical description, but it should be attributed to contemporary critic V. G. Chertkov or clearly presented as analysis rather than as statutory wording.

## 4. State-policy context — Circular No.237 was not an isolated clerical clarification

A. I. Savin's archival reconstruction using Anti-Religious Commission records closes an important causal context:
- 28 Nov 1922: the Commission took the position that the military-duty privilege should be limited to sects that had enjoyed it before the revolution;
- 26 Jun 1923: the Commission pursued propaganda, legal and operative measures around alleged abuse of the exemption regime;
- 31 Oct 1923: the Commission approved the Circular No.237 line;
- 5 Nov 1923: NKJu/Supreme Court circular issued;
- the circular appeared immediately before the XXV Baptist congress, whose military question was under active state/security scrutiny.

Safe synthesis:
> Circular No.237 was both a judicial-administrative rule and part of a broader state policy of narrowing access to conscientious objection and pressuring evangelical institutions on the military question.

Do not write that the circular alone proves the outcome of every individual court case.

## 6. Article-by-article control, 1925 law

### Article 216 — eligibility

Citizens could be exempted by provincial/equivalent courts if, **by birth and upbringing**, they belonged to families attached to sects whose religious teaching prohibited armed military service **at the present time and before 1917**.

This is a statutory continuation of the historical/confessional gate visible in Circular No.237.

Status: **PRIMARY TEXT CLOSED**.

### Article 217 — evidence / expertise / appeal

Courts could invite experts and obtain opinions from competent institutions/persons. Court rulings could be appealed in the ordinary way.

Important correction to older Chapter 10 language:
- the 1920 removal of OSROG's monopoly should not be rewritten as `no expertise`;
- by 1925 expert evidence still exists, but it is court-controlled rather than monopolized by one religious council.

Status: **PRIMARY TEXT CLOSED**.

### Article 218 — timing gate

The favorable court ruling had to be presented to the relevant draft commission inside the statutory pre-conscription window.

Production prose should state the existence of the timing gate; quote exact window only from the canonical text/version being cited because later amendments changed military-law implementation.

Status: **PRIMARY TEXT CLOSED / VERSION-SENSITIVE WORDING**.

### Article 219 — no timely ruling = ordinary conscription

A claimant asserting religious objections but lacking the required timely court decision was called to compulsory military service on the ordinary basis with peers.

Meaning:
> conscience alone had no automatic suspensive effect.

Status: **PRIMARY TEXT CLOSED**.

### Article 220 — socially useful work

Those exempted for religious reasons and medically fit were assigned to epidemic-control work or other socially useful work, including examples such as fighting forest fires/epizootics and earthworks.

This continues the substitution principle rather than full unconditional release.

Status: **PRIMARY TEXT CLOSED**.

### Article 221 — production handling

Article 221 must be mapped from the exact original 1925 publication before a verbatim chapter quote is used. Later consolidated versions can silently include amendments.

Rule:
> cite original 1925 publication for the chapter's 1925 legal state; use consolidated versions only as discovery/control copies and label amendments by date.

## 7. Baptist-specific consequence of the legal gate

The 1905/1906 Russian Baptist confession contained an Article 13 formula affirming military duty when required by government. Therefore a Baptist claimant's personal pacifism could collide with a state test asking whether the sect's teaching prohibited armed service before 1917.

This does **not** prove automatic statutory exclusion of every Baptist. It does prove that the legal test was especially difficult for a postwar Baptist pacifist whose personal conviction differed from the earlier official confessional text.

Safe prose:
> `The Soviet legal test increasingly asked not only what this believer believed, but what his family and denomination could prove they had believed before the revolution.`
