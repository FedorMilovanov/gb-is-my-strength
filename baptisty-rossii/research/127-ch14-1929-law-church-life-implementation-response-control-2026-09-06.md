## 0. Purpose

The statute alone cannot tell the Baptist story. This file controls the second half of Chapter 14:

> **What was already happening before 8 April, what Baptists actually experienced during 1929, what they said in response, and what can/cannot be causally attributed to the new law.**

The chapter must keep four timelines distinct:
1. pre-law anti-religious pressure;
2. 8 Apr statutory change;
3. 18 May constitutional change;
4. subsequent administrative/political enforcement and Baptist institutional contraction.

Rule:
> **Chronological proximity is not proof of direct causation.**

## 16. Final implementation verdict

Chapter 14 can now avoid the two equally weak extremes:

> `в 1929 всё запретили`  
> versus  
> `ничего особенного юридически не произошло`.

The evidence supports a more exact story:

> **The Soviet state did not abolish all religious worship in one stroke. It legally narrowed a living Baptist movement into a tightly supervised local-cult model while political and administrative pressure was already dismantling education, publishing, federation, itinerant mission and leadership networks. Baptist communities continued to meet, organize and protest during 1929, but the institutional space in which they could lawfully act became radically smaller.**

That is the handoff into Chapter 15: the legal framework is now in place; the next chapter must document how the 1930s repression operated through arrests, closures, exile, camps and the destruction of organizational continuity.
