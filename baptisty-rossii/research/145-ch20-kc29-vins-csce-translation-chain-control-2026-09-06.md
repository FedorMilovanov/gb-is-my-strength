# A. KC.29 — VINS 1979 IS BOUNDED TO EXACT FOLDERS

## 1. Collection

- Keston Center, Baylor University;
- `Soviet Union Unregistered Baptists Subject Files`;
- accession **KC.29**;
- 32 A4 document boxes.

The finding aid states that Keston collected, verified, translated and disseminated information from believers behind the Iron Curtain.

It also states an important arrangement rule:
- material received in an existing category/file order generally retained that order;
- unordered material was later arranged thematically and chronologically.

Therefore folder adjacency can guide research, but cannot automatically prove original transmission sequence.

# G. BACK-TRANSLATION BAN — NOW OBJECT-SPECIFIC

## 8. No synthetic Russian quotation

If an English translation has a compelling sentence that is hard to read/find in the Russian facsimile, do not:
- translate the English sentence back into Russian;
- put quotation marks around the back-translation;
- cite it as Vins's original Russian wording.

Instead use either:
- the verified Russian original;
- or an explicitly labelled paraphrase of the English translation.

This is no longer only a general rule: Box 30 Folder 5 is the first concrete object to which the rule applies.

# H. RIGHTS / REPRODUCTION

## 9. Baylor rights metadata matters

For the digital Russian issue, Baylor identifies the Council of Churches of Evangelical Christian Baptists as rights holder and links to Baylor use policies.

Therefore:
- `digitized and publicly viewable` ≠ `free production reuse`;
- screenshot/page reproduction for the book remains a rights question;
- textual factual use and limited quotation require separate copyright analysis.

KC.29 finding aid also states:
- copying requests are submitted to the Keston Center;
- some unpublished creator-produced materials are described as public domain;
- copyright for other materials may be unknown;
- users remain responsible for lawful use.

No blanket `Keston archive = public domain` claim is allowed.
