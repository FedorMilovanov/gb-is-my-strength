## 0. Why this file exists

A recurring narrative about 1942–1944 says, in effect:

> `Власть выпустила из лагерей руководителей евангельских христиан и баптистов, чтобы они создали единый управляемый Союз.`

There may be real state involvement in the return/use of particular religious leaders, but this sentence is too strong as a blanket claim.

It compresses at least four distinct questions:
1. when each leader was actually arrested and released;
2. whether release was normal, early, administrative, wartime-directive based or individually ordered;
3. when each person resumed church work;
4. whether a document links that person's release to a plan to create/use a national religious centre.

Temporal proximity is not proof of motive.

---

# A. KAREV — chronology correction

## 1. Alexander V. Karev was not released in 1942

A family-based biography preserves a letter from Karev dated **8 Mar 1940** in which he told his wife he had been released and was making plans for the future.

It further states that after returning to Moscow he resumed church work and travelled to congregations.

Public biography:
- `https://volamar.ru/subject/01zerno/text/karev/karev.html`

Moscow Central Church retrospective independently says:
- Karev had been arrested in 1935;
- the last imprisonment lasted five years;
- after release he worked in the Moscow church and visited churches.

Source:
- `https://mbchurch.ru/publications/articles/15/11772/`

Orthodox Encyclopedia's Moscow history also gives the 1935 imprisonment line.

### Correction

Reject:
> `Карев был освобожден из лагеря в 1942 году для участия во Временном совете.`

Controlled chronology:
> **Karev was already free by March 1940 and had resumed church work before the 1942 combined-centre phase.**

This disproves the undifferentiated formulation that `Zhidkov and Karev were both released in 1942 to create the union`.

Status: **1940 RELEASE CHRONOLOGY STRONG / EXACT ARCHIVAL RELEASE ORDER STILL OPEN**.

---

## 2. Later recollection must not override the dated 1940 letter

A later recollection by M. Ya. Zhidkov is often quoted roughly as:

> `В сорок втором году мой отец вернулся. Он и Карев вернулся...`

Read casually, this can sound as though both men returned in 1942.

But Karev's family chronology contains a dated March 1940 release letter.

Therefore:
- explicit `1942` securely applies to Zhidkov's return;
- it must not be used to redated Karev's release to 1942 against the earlier dated family document.

Production rule:
> where a memoir sentence is grammatically ambiguous, independently dated events control chronology.

# B. ZHIDKOV — 1942 early release is real

## 3. Ya. I. Zhidkov repression/release spine

A research publication summarizing archival work gives:
- arrested **3 Jan 1938**;
- sentenced in May 1938 to eight years ITL;
- served in the Kolyma system;
- **released early on 29 Apr 1942**.

Research publication:
- Denis Samarin study, public PDF: `https://www.denis-samarin.ru/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/60159315.pdf`.

Later biographical/denominational sources agree on:
- imprisonment 1938–1942;
- return in 1942.

### Current verdict

Strong:
> `Жидков был досрочно освобожден 29 апреля 1942 года.`

Not yet proven:
> `Жидкова освободили специально для руководства объединенным евангельско-баптистским центром.`

The second sentence requires a motive/decision document.

---

## 4. 29 Apr 1942 is a dangerous date for causal inference

There was also a wartime NKVD/Prosecutor directive dated **29 Apr 1942** concerning release/retention/employment categories of prisoners. Later implementation documents refer to `директива №185 от 29 апреля 1942 г.`.

This coincidence is research-significant but **not proof** that Zhidkov was released under that general directive.

Reasons to hold:
- different prisoner categories were treated differently;
- political/security-category prisoners could be excluded from ordinary release/mobilization routes;
- the exact legal basis in Zhidkov's personal release file has not yet been recovered.

Production ban:
> do not write `Жидкова освободили по директиве №185` without his release paper/file saying so.

P0:
- Zhidkov release certificate/order;
- camp personal file;
- NKVD/prosecutor correspondence if exceptional decision was used.

# C. RETURN TO CHURCH WORK ≠ IMMEDIATE LEADERSHIP PERMISSION

## 5. Zhidkov's post-release church access

M. Ya. Zhidkov's recollection says that after his father's 1942 return, he and Karev were initially not permitted to preach from the pulpit, later resuming public preaching after a period of months.

This shows an intermediate state:

```text
released / physically in Moscow
        ≠
fully restored public church authority
```

Need distinguish:
- freedom from confinement;
- residence permission;
- attendance at worship;
- preaching permission/practice;
- official representation of a union;
- travel/visitation authorization.

The exact authority responsible for the temporary pulpit restriction is not yet established.

Safe:
> `Семейное свидетельство Жидкова вспоминает, что после возвращения публичное проповедническое служение возобновилось не сразу.`

Do not infer:
> `НКВД официально запретил Жидкову проповедовать шесть месяцев` without an order.

# D. ORLOV AND ANDREEV — continuity matters

## 6. Not every 1942 leader was a newly released prisoner

Orthodox Encyclopedia's Moscow history states that by early 1938, after repression of other leaders, **A. L. Andreev and M. A. Orlov remained free** in VSEKh leadership, and Orlov chaired VSEKh in 1938–1944.

This makes the phrase:
> `руководителей нового центра освободили из лагерей в 1942`

structurally false as a description of the group.

Some leadership continuity survived outside prison.

Narrative consequence:
> 1942 coordination was built from a mixture of surviving legal leadership, previously repressed/released leaders and Baptist representatives — not one batch of newly released prisoners.

# F. `MOST 1944 DELEGATES WERE RECENT PRISONERS`

## 8. Treat as a quantification claim, not rhetoric

Some later reference works describe the October 1944 participants as largely/recently released from imprisonment.

That may reflect real repression history of the leadership stratum, but `most of 45 delegates` is a measurable claim.

It requires:
- the 45-person delegate list;
- person-by-person repression status;
- definition of `recently`;
- release dates;
- distinction between prison, camp, exile, administrative deportation and no repression.

Current status: **HOLD**.

Do not write:
> `большинство 45 делегатов только что вышло из лагерей`.

Interim:
> `среди руководителей и участников были люди с недавним опытом заключения и ссылки.`

Even that should be illustrated with named closed cases, not anonymous magnitude.

# G. STATE MOTIVE — what we actually have

## 9. July 1944 memo is strong state-policy evidence

Polyansky's 24 Jul 1944 memo proves that by that date SDRK wanted formal organization of the combined centre and gave policy reasons:
- practical governance;
- Baptist dissatisfaction, especially in Ukraine;
- wartime/liberated-territory situation;
- patriotic activity;
- foreign contacts.

This is strong evidence for **state interest in formalization**.

It is not retroactive proof that:
- every 1940/1942 release was engineered for that formalization;
- the state had a fixed 1944 union blueprint at the moment each earlier prisoner was released.

Causal chronology matters.

---

## 10. Earliest document wins over later teleology

To claim:
> `Person X was released in order to help create the united centre`

we need a source created at or before release, or an official later record directly describing that decision.

Later outcome alone is insufficient:

```text
released in 1942
      +
leader in combined council in 1942/44
      ≠
released because state planned council
```

This is the central anti-teleology rule for this file.

# 15. Narrative verdict

Not:
> `The Soviet state released the leaders and ordered them to unite.`

But:
> **The new centre was assembled from people with very different wartime statuses: some had remained in the surviving legal leadership, Karev had returned from imprisonment before the war's middle phase, Zhidkov was released early in April 1942, and Baptist representatives entered joint work in May. The state clearly became an active architect of the permitted centralized form, but the motive and mechanism of each earlier release must be proved separately.**

This keeps the chapter documentary rather than teleological.
