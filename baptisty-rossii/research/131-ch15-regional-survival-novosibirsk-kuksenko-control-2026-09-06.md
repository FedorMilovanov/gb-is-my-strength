## 0. Why this file exists

Chapter 15 must not imply a single national sequence:

> `1929 law → all churches immediately closed → 1937 all leaders shot → nothing remained until 1944`.

That is too flat and often false at local level.

The actual evidence shows asynchronous regional trajectories:

- some unions lost central infrastructure before local congregations disappeared;
- some congregations lost their own building but continued in another registered room/building;
- some communities survived by concentrating into one remaining legal centre;
- some continued through domestic / semi-legal / clandestine meetings;
- some local networks remained active long enough to become targets in the 1937–1938 mass operations;
- individual leaders could be arrested, released, exiled, return, and be arrested again under a different mechanism.

Chapter 15 therefore needs a **regional survival matrix**, not one national shutdown date.

## 3. The Kukhman case: what archival scholarship actually establishes

A. I. Savin's archival work on O. I. Kukhman is the strongest current research bridge.

Key publication:

- Савин А. И. `Трагедия евангельских христиан. «Дело» пресвитера О. И. Кухмана (1937 г.)` // *Книга памяти жертв политических репрессий Новосибирской области*. Новосибирск, 2005. Вып.1. С.394–404.

Related archival study:

- Савин А. И. `Репрессии в отношении евангельских верующих в ходе «кулацкой» операции НКВД` // *Сталинизм в советской провинции: 1937–1938. Массовая операция на основе приказа №00447*. М.: РОССПЭН, 2009. С.303–342.

Useful public scholarly reproduction:
- https://zaimka.ru/savin-baptist-congregation/

### Controlled sequence

Savin's reconstruction shows:

1. O. I. Kukhman, the regional Evangelical Christian representative, was arrested **14 Apr 1937**, before the formal start of the mass operation under Order 00447.
2. The investigation extracted accusations against active leaders of Novosibirsk evangelical communities.
3. For the **Baptist** part of the constructed case, one cited factual contact was that **F. P. Kuksenko**, leader of the Novosibirsk Baptist congregation, had asked Kukhman in autumn 1936 for permission to use the Evangelical Christian prayer house for Baptist meetings.
4. The state converted ordinary inter-congregational religious contact into evidence of a supposed counter-revolutionary bloc.
5. In Apr–Jun 1937, 26 Baptists, Evangelical Christians and Seventh-day Adventists were arrested/processed in the Novosibirsk Kukhman case.
6. On **28 Aug 1937** the UNKVD troika for West Siberian Krai sentenced the 26 under arts.58-10/11; **18 received death sentences**.

This is a high-value narrative mechanism:

> a request to share a prayer house becomes, in the logic of the investigation, a link in an invented conspiratorial network.

### Essential wording control

Narrator must write:

> `Следствие использовало межобщинные связи как доказательство предполагаемого «контрреволюционного блока».`

Not:

> `Куксенко и Кухман создали контрреволюционный блок.`

The latter merely reproduces the accusation as historical fact.

## 5. Do not use `793` as a Baptist death/repression count

Savin cites an NKVD report giving **793 repressed** in 1937 in the alleged `шпионско-диверсионная организация среди сектантов Сибкрая`.

This number is **not**:

- 793 Baptists;
- 793 members of the Novosibirsk Baptist congregation;
- 793 executions;
- 793 people in Kuksenko's local case.

The constructed umbrella included multiple evangelical/protestant groups and people across different localities.

Production rule:

> `793` may appear only as the reported scale of the broader NKVD-created “sectarian” case complex, with explicit scope and source attribution.

Otherwise omit it.

## 6. Slavgorod proves that network repression was geographically extended

Savin's Slavgorod study gives an important second regional witness.

Controlled points:

- S. V. Petrov had been a member of the Novosibirsk Baptist congregation in 1935–1936 and knew F. P. Kuksenko and O. I. Kukhman;
- after travelling among Baptist communities he was presented by investigators as an emissary of the alleged wider organization;
- in Slavgorod, religious preaching, Bibles and religious literature were incorporated into the accusation structure;
- **26 Sep 1937** the West Siberian UNKVD troika sentenced nine Baptists to death, three to ten years and two to eight years in one group;
- a separately spun-off `баптисты на элеваторе` case produced additional death sentences.

Archival references exposed by Savin include:

- `ОСД УАДАК. Ф. Р-2. Оп.7. Д.5861`, including ll.1, 88–94, 112–114;
- additional West Siberian case references in the article.

### Narrative value

This demonstrates how the investigative model expanded:

```text
real congregation network
    ↓
travel / preaching / mutual aid / shared premises
    ↓
state reinterpretation as organizational links
    ↓
constructed regional conspiracy
    ↓
separate local group cases
```

Do not narrate this as proof that the alleged conspiracy existed.

# B. MOSCOW — CONCENTRATION INTO ONE LEGAL CENTRE

## 7. Moscow is the counterexample to `everything closed`

The Moscow line is crucial because it demonstrates survival by concentration.

Orthodox Encyclopedia's Moscow entry states:

- in May 1930 the Baptist Union house at **1st Brestskaya, 29** was confiscated; the Union office, Bible courses and workers' apartments were located there;
- in **1929–1932** almost all Evangelical Christian and Baptist prayer houses in Moscow were closed;
- the central Evangelical Christian church in **Maly Vuzovsky Lane** remained open;
- after 1931 the central organizational structure no longer had its prior press and itinerant evangelists;
- after arrests, only a very small leadership residue remained free by the late 1930s.

Public reference:
- https://pravenc.ru/text/2564186.html

The surviving central church later also hosted other displaced Protestant groups.

### Production formulation

Safe:
> `В Москве почти все евангельские молитвенные дома закрыли, но один центральный легальный узел продолжал действовать.`

Unsafe:
> `К 1932 году протестантских церквей в Москве не осталось.`

This surviving node matters for the bridge to 1942–1944.

---

## 8. Survival ≠ normal freedom

The fact that one Moscow congregation remained open must not be used to neutralize the wider repression.

Its survival occurred while:

- other prayer houses were closed;
- union infrastructure was confiscated;
- leadership was arrested;
- office/support spaces were taken;
- other Protestant congregations were pushed into the remaining legal site;
- the institutional field was drastically narrowed.

Chapter language should therefore use:

> `сохранился ограниченный легальный узел`

rather than:

> `религиозная свобода сохранялась`.

# E. SURVIVAL MODES

## 12. Canonical survival taxonomy for Chapter 15

Use these modes instead of a binary `open/closed` model.

### S1 — registered legal congregation in own building

Example type:
- surviving legal central node.

Need exact registration/building proof.

### S2 — registered/legal congregation after relocation

Example type:
- Leningrad congregation after loss of House of Gospel.

Building loss does not equal immediate congregation extinction.

### S3 — concentration/absorption into another surviving legal congregation

Example type:
- believers from closed Moscow-area communities attending the remaining central congregation.

### S4 — domestic / informal / clandestine religious meeting network

Use only where a source explicitly describes home/illegal meetings.

Do not infer clandestinity merely because the legal union had disappeared.

### S5 — dispersed believer/family network without stable congregational evidence

Useful where biographies show continued pastoral care, parcels, visits, family prayer and contact but no controlled congregation status.

### S6 — institutional continuity through surviving leaders

Example:
- remnants of ВСЕХ leadership continuing in highly constrained form;
- later 1942 transition should be handled in Chapter 16, not prematurely narrated here.

## 13. What `survival` does not mean

Survival does not automatically imply:

- legal registration;
- a prayer house;
- a functioning regional union;
- public evangelism;
- children/youth work;
- legal literature distribution;
- freedom from surveillance;
- freedom from arrests.

Every survival claim needs its own object:

```text
surviving people ≠ surviving congregation ≠ surviving building ≠ surviving union
```

# F. THE `FOUR PRAYER HOUSES` CLAIM

## 14. Do not canonize the shortcut yet

Older research repeats a formulation that on the eve of the war only four Evangelical Christian/Baptist prayer houses remained open — often listed as Moscow, Leningrad, Novosibirsk and Ulyanovsk.

Current Chapter 15 status: **HOLD**.

Reasons:

1. scope can vary — RSFSR vs USSR, denomination vs combined evangelical field;
2. `prayer house` may not equal `registered congregation`;
3. one source can count buildings while another counts communities;
4. local reopenings/relocations can create chronology problems;
5. the exact underlying administrative dataset has not yet been recovered.

Production must not use:

> `К 1941 году в СССР осталось четыре баптистских церкви.`

That sentence compresses multiple uncertain dimensions into one false precision.

Allowed interim wording:

> `К концу 1930-х сеть легальных молитвенных домов была сведена к крайне малому числу, при этом точный общесоюзный подсчёт требует отдельной проверки.`
