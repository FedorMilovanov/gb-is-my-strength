## 1. Central thesis

Safe synthesis:

> International contacts reopened by famine relief quickly became channels for reconstruction: theological training, Bible publication, periodicals and national organizational work. Baptists and Evangelical Christians repeatedly attempted to share this infrastructure, but common faith and foreign encouragement did not erase disputes over representation, control and union identity. The 1924 joint Bible-course experiment therefore belongs beside the later separate Leningrad and Moscow schools: together they show both the reality of cooperation and the failure to create one durable educational institution.

Forbidden simplifications:
- `the two unions merged educationally in 1924`;
- `the 1924 school was exclusively Prokhanov's project`;
- `Baptists had a full independent Moscow Bible school already in 1926`;
- `all Bible/College Fund money was famine relief`;
- `foreign Baptists simply dictated a merger`;
- `all 1926–1928 publishing numbers in late histories are already primary-page verified`;
- `1928 was already the 1929 legal shutdown`.

## 4. 1924 joint Bible-course experiment

Multiple later/internal sources agree on a common Baptist/Evangelical Christian course in Leningrad in 1924.

Stronger contemporary archive trail:
- Walter Oliver Lewis Collection finding aid explicitly lists **3 Apr 1924** report of the Committee of All-Russia's Baptist Union on Bible courses in Leningrad.
- Jan 1925 educational correspondence specifically revisits Baptist participation in Bible courses at the Leningrad Evangelical Church.

MRW 1925 indexed visual/text witness:
- caption: Leningrad Bible School of Evangelical Christians;
- principal: I. S. Prokhanov;
- term: **1 Jan–1 Jul 1924**;
- accompanying text: **37 pupils** during previous school year.

Important terminology gate:
- Russian later histories often call the course `объединённые библейские курсы`;
- MRW caption names the school by the Evangelical Christian institution.

Production rule:
> Describe 1924 as a **joint educational experiment hosted within the Leningrad Evangelical Christian institutional setting**, with Baptist participation documented independently; do not rewrite it as a fully merged denominational seminary.

Status: **JOINT-COURSE CORE STRONG / EXACT ROSTER+FUNDING+CURRICULUM OPEN**.

## 6. Bible publishing is reconstruction infrastructure, not a side note

- `Братский Вестник` №3, 1957 reports:
  - Leningrad canonical Russian Bible, 1926, 25,000 copies;
  - smaller-format Kiev Bible, 1927, 10,000;

- Russian Bible Society history confirms Prokhanov's Soviet-orthography Scripture publishing project and identifies 25,000 New Testaments and a 35,000-copy Bible project in the 1926–1928 period.

There are inconsistencies among later summaries over exact year/title/count combinations.
