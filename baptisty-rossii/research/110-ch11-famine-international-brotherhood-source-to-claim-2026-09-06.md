## 1. Central thesis

Safe synthesis:

> The famine of 1921–1922 turned pre-existing evangelical migration, missionary and denominational contacts into practical transnational relief networks. Russian believers were not passive recipients of a Western rescue: emigrant Russian and Ukrainian Baptists raised money, Russian church leaders supplied appeals and local distribution knowledge, American and European Baptist bodies mobilized large shipments and funding, and the American Relief Administration provided a crucial transport/distribution infrastructure. Relief strengthened a sense of international Baptist brotherhood, but it also created new demands for accountability, representation and cooperation between the separate Russian Baptist and Evangelical Christian unions.

The chapter therefore has two connected narratives:
1. **hunger → relief → local survival**;
2. **relief → transnational infrastructure → stronger institutional relationships and pressures**.

Forbidden simplifications:
- `America saved the Russian Baptists`;
- `all foreign aid went only to Baptists`;
- `ARA was a Baptist organization`;
- `foreign Baptist money caused the Russian revival`;
- `the famine automatically united Baptists and Evangelical Christians`;
- `every exact dollar figure in later histories is already primary-facsimile verified`;
- `relief and missionary/Bible funds were the same accounting stream`.

## 5. ARA as infrastructure, not denominational owner of the work

### Diaspora food-draft route

Potapova cites:
- I. Ya. Bokmelder, `О помощи голодающим в России`, `Сеятель истины`, Jan 1922 №1 p.10.

The described mechanism uses the American Relief Administration's warehouse/food-draft system rather than expensive private food parcels.

Neprash reportedly explored going to Russia through ARA but was unable to be accepted under the relevant restrictions because of citizenship/status; exact wording is located at `Сеятель истины`, June 1922 №6 p.15.

Status: **PRIMARY LOCATORS STRONG / FACSIMILE OPEN**.

### Northern Baptist shipment route

American Baptist Foreign Mission Society Annual Report 1923 directly reports:
- a renewed relief campaign after the 1921 shipment;
- nearly 7,000 packages received for the second campaign;
- shipment totaling **1,091 bales of clothing, 8 bales of children's clothing, and 26 cases of miscellaneous supplies**;
- transport from New York on an ARA steamship without ocean freight cost to the denomination;
- W. O. Lewis instructed to meet the shipment and cooperate with ARA;
- Soviet inspection/unpacking before release;
- Moscow conference with J. H. Rushbrooke and creation of an **all-Russian Baptist-Evangelical relief committee**;
- distribution through multiple centers;
- a substantial portion distributed under the Hoover agreement through ARA to recipients **irrespective of religious affiliation**.

These figures belong to this specific ABFMS shipment, not to all famine relief.

## 6. Russian local agency in distribution

The ABFMS report is crucial because it does not describe a purely foreign distribution machine. It says an all-Russian Baptist-Evangelical relief committee was organized in Moscow and a multi-center distribution program was prepared.

Potapova's `Сеятель истины` reconstruction separately reports that Southern Baptist representative Everett Gill consulted Russian Baptist leaders, including the Pavlovs, and adopted distributed local committees/centers rather than one central handout point.

Safe synthesis:
> Foreign supplies entered through international organizations, but Russian evangelical leaders and congregational geography were part of the distribution architecture.

Do not yet identify the complete membership of the all-Russian relief committee without its protocol or list.

## 7. Southern Baptists — relief opens a renewed institutional contact

The Southern Baptist Convention's 1922 annual reporting says:

## 8. Relief creates a broader international Baptist infrastructure

European Baptist Federation's historical chronology records:
- 1919 Rushbrooke and C. A. Brooks toured Central/Eastern Europe to assess postwar needs;
- 1920 London conference organized international Baptist help/relief and development work;
- Rushbrooke was appointed BWA Europe Commissioner.

Thus the Russian famine response did not arise ex nihilo in 1921. It plugged into a postwar Baptist relief infrastructure already being built across Europe.

## 9. Relief and unity become entangled

Contemporary ABFMS 1923 reporting shows two processes in the same institutional environment:
1. very large relief activity in Russia under Rushbrooke/Lewis/ARA cooperation;
2. explicit foreign Baptist approval of a proposed union between Russian Baptists and Evangelical Christians (BWA Executive Committee 1 Aug 1922; ABFMS Board 10 Oct 1922).

A separate archive-based Disciples-history source indicates that foreign relief/mission grants could be tied to expectations of representative joint administration and greater cooperation between the Russian unions.

Safe synthesis:
> Relief strengthened practical interdependence and made the organizational fragmentation of Russian evangelical Christianity visible to foreign partners. Some foreign agencies therefore encouraged — and in some cases conditioned future support on — more representative joint structures.

Do not write:
- `foreigners ordered the two Russian unions to merge`;
- `money bought the 1920s unity negotiations`;
- `all aid was conditional`.

Status: **INSTITUTIONAL RELATIONSHIP STRONG / INDIVIDUAL CONDITIONALITY DOCUMENTS NEED DIRECT FACSIMILE**.

## 11. Stockholm 1923 — international brotherhood becomes public

### Canonical date correction

Official Baptist World Alliance bibliography:
> Third Baptist World Congress, Stockholm, **21–27 July 1923**.

MASTER contains both June and July date captions. `21–27 June` is a caption conflict and must not be used. Correct canonical date: **21–27 Jul 1923**.

### Official relief resolution

BWA Resolution 1923.3, `Thanks for Help in Time of Distress`, pp.57–58 of the original congress record:
- thanks Baptist groups/countries that supplied food and clothing;
- explicitly mentions feeding the hungry, clothing the naked, helping widows/orphans;
- explicitly says aid also reached needy people outside Baptist ranks;
- names Rushbrooke's work gathering information and intelligently directing aid.

This is the strongest official expression of the chapter's “international brotherhood” theme.
