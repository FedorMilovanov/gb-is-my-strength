## 0. Why this file exists

Chapter 20 already uses the Keston/Baylor archive to show how Soviet Baptist testimony crossed the information border.

A major methodological risk is to let that international archive become a one-sided history in which:

```text
unregistered / Council of Churches Baptists = authentic testimony
registered / AUCECB Baptists = only official silence
```

That binary does not survive the archive structure itself.

Baylor's **KC.22 — Soviet Union Registered Baptists Subject Files** contains:
- official registered-Baptist publications;
- internal/independent texts;
- material on state interference and imprisonment;
- samizdat;
- foreign/ecumenical contacts;
- church statistics and organizational records.

The existence of these lanes does not automatically prove every claim inside them. It does prove that a balanced Chapter 20 must inspect the registered-Baptist archive as an independent evidence stream.

# G. ARCHIVE BALANCE CONTRACT

## 7. Minimum four-voice late-Soviet archive set

For disputed 1970s–1980s claims, Chapter 20 should, when relevant, seek:

1. **unregistered internal voice** — KC.29 / Council of Churches / prisoner-family / samizdat;
2. **registered internal voice** — KC.22 official publication or registered-Baptist document;
3. **state voice** — Soviet law, CRA/SDRK records, court/police/party documents;
4. **external reception voice** — Keston reporting, CSCE, BWA, foreign government/church archive.

This is not a quota.

It is an anti-bias rule for claims about:
- which Baptist branch represented Soviet believers;
- persecution magnitude;
- foreign legitimacy;
- religious freedom;
- church-state accommodation/resistance.
