# 114. Chapter 11 → Chapter 12 boundary: relief, Bible, college and reconstruction funds

Дата прохода: 2026-09-06  
Authority: `docs/BAPTISTY-ROSSII-BOOK-AUTHORITY-V2.md`  
Статус: **EDITORIAL BOUNDARY CLOSED / INDIVIDUAL FUND LEDGERS PARTIAL**

## 0. Problem

In 1922–1924 the same international Baptist actors often appear in records about:
- famine relief;
- clothing/food distribution;
- Bibles and New Testaments;
- college/seminary funds;
- missionary support;
- union negotiations.

Without an explicit boundary, Chapter 11 and Chapter 12 will duplicate sources and inflate or mislabel money.

---

## 1. Chapter 11 owns emergency relief

Chapter 11 keeps:
- famine appeals;
- food/clothing collections;
- ARA food drafts / transport / distribution;
- famine relief committees;
- local distribution architecture;
- relief to non-Baptists;
- international Baptist fellowship created/strengthened through emergency aid;
- administrative pressure for representative joint relief structures when directly tied to aid.

Examples:
- Neprash's Dec1921 famine appeal and 1922 donation reports;
- Southern Baptist physical relief / Everett Gill;
- ABFMS clothing shipment and all-Russian relief committee;
- John Johnson / Disciples famine-relief committee;
- BWA 1923 `Thanks for Help in Time of Distress`.

---

## 2. Chapter 12 owns reconstruction infrastructure

Chapter 12 should own:
- Russian Bible Fund;
- Bible/New Testament printing/distribution projects after the emergency relief narrative is established;
- College Fund / theological education;
- Bible institutes/courses/schools;
- publishing infrastructure;
- institutional attempts to create shared educational structures;
- 1923–1928 failed unity as it intersects education/print/governance.

Emergency relief can be recalled briefly as the network precondition, not retold.

---

## 3. Southern/Northern Baptist $14,000 Bible appropriation

Contemporary Southern Baptist reporting states:
- Southern Baptist board appropriated $7,000 for Russian Bibles/New Testaments;
- American Baptist Foreign Mission Society appropriated a like $7,000;
- reported combined purchasing power exceeded 100,000 Bibles/Testaments under favorable European exchange conditions.

Classification:
> **BIBLE / MISSION INFRASTRUCTURE, NOT FAMINE-RELIEF CASH.**

Chapter 11 may mention that famine-era contact reopened channels through which Bible projects also moved.
Chapter 12 owns the money, quantities and distribution claim.

## 4. BWA College Fund — 1923 Stockholm handoff

Official BWA World Congress Resolution 1923.6 `Russian Delegates`:
- welcomes Russian delegates;
- states that conditions appear more favorable for carrying out the **original purpose of the College Fund**;
- refers the matter and Dr. Cree's proposals to the Executive Committee for further counsel with the relevant boards.

Original citation:
- W. T. Whitley, ed., *Third Baptist World Congress: Stockholm, July 21–27, 1923*;
- minutes p. xxviii;
- circumstances also referenced at p.195 by the official BWA digital edition.

Classification:
> **CHAPTER 12 EDUCATION / INSTITUTIONAL RECONSTRUCTION.**

Chapter 11 uses this resolution only as its final transition:
> International networks that had carried relief were now being asked to carry reconstruction capital and educational plans.

## 5. Neprash fund separation

Wardin finding aid itself distinguishes:
- 89.23 Neprash correspondence/reports, 1921–1922;
- 91.16 Russian Bible Fund – I. V. Neprash;
- 91.17 Russian College Fund;
- 91.18–91.20 Russian famine folders.

This archival classification is a warning against collapsing all Neprash-associated money into one stream.

Production rule:
- every Neprash dollar claim must receive a `fund_purpose` label;
- famine donations must not be added to Bible Fund totals;
- Bible Fund must not be added to College Fund;
- general union/mission cash requires its own category.

---

## 6. Relief committee ≠ school/college committee

The all-Russian Baptist-Evangelical relief committee reported by ABFMS 1923 is a relief/distribution body.

Do not assume:
- it administered the College Fund;
- it was the same committee contemplated for theological education;
- it represented a formal merger of the two Russian unions.

Any shared personnel must be documented, not inferred into institutional identity.

---

## 7. 1922 union pressure: split by function

Foreign Baptist approval of Russian Baptist/Evangelical union in 1922 can appear in both chapters with different functions:

### Chapter 11 use
Relief/accountability question:
> divided Russian representation complicated receipt, distribution and accountability for international help.

### Chapter 12 use
Reconstruction/education question:
> foreign partners also preferred coherent structures for theological school, college, Bible and mission support.

Do not duplicate the full negotiation chronology in both chapters.

## 8. Stockholm 1923 editorial hinge

Chapter 11 closing scene:
1. Russian delegation appears at global Congress after famine emergency.
2. Congress publicly remembers international relief (`Resolution 1923.3`).
3. Congress also reactivates the Russian College Fund question (`Resolution 1923.6`).

This creates a natural documentary hinge:
> **from emergency brotherhood to reconstruction infrastructure.**

Then Chapter 12 starts with Bible, education and the institutional question of who would control/represent the rebuilt work.

- `purpose` = `famine_food`, `clothing_relief`, `refugee_relief`, `bible_printing`, `college`, `seminary`, `general_mission`, `other`;
- `amount`;
- `currency`;
- `reporting_period`;
- `cumulative_or_periodic`;
- `sender`;
- `recipient`;
- `intermediary`;
- `source_issue/page`;
- `receipt_status`;
- `overlap_notes`.

No sum enters narrative aggregation without `cumulative_or_periodic` and `overlap_notes` resolution.
