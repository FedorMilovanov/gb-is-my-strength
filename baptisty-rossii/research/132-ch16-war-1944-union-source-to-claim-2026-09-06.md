## 0. Editorial thesis

Chapter 16 must reject both monocausal stories:

> `Государство решило объединить баптистов и евангельских христиан — и создало ВСЕХБ.`

and

> `Два братства совершенно независимо от государства решили объединиться в 1944 году.`

The controlled documentary model is:

> **wartime catastrophe and patriotic mobilization change the practical field → a surviving Evangelical Christian centre and Baptist leaders begin joint work in 1942 → local cooperation and visitation expand as territory is liberated → the Soviet state creates a new administrative channel for non-Orthodox religions in May 1944 → M. A. Orlov formally petitions for permission to convene an all-Union meeting in July → I. V. Polyansky supports the petition for explicitly state-policy reasons → 45 delegates meet in Moscow on 26–29 Oct 1944 and unanimously establish one Union and one All-Union Council.**

The chapter therefore has **two distinguishable causal lines**:

```text
church / brotherhood line                 state-policy line
-------------------------                 -----------------
pre-1929 unity attempts                   wartime religious-policy shift
surviving relationships                   administrative need on liberated territory
May 1942 Baptist approach to VSEKh        May 1944 SDRK
joint wartime work                        July 1944 Polyansky support
local cooperation                         permission / oversight
                \                         /
                 \                       /
                  26–29 Oct 1944 meeting
                           ↓
                  Union EC + Baptists
                           ↓
                         VSEKhIB
```

Neither line should erase the other.

## 2. A1/A2-02 — Polyansky memo to Molotov, 24 Jul 1944

Document title in published collection:

`Докладная записка Председателя Совета по делам религиозных культов при СНК СССР И.В. Полянского заместителю Председателя СНК СССР В.М. Молотову по вопросам деятельности ВСЕХБ`.

Date:
- **24 Jul 1944**;
- No.11;
- `Секретно`.

Archival pointer:
- **ГАРФ. Ф. Р-6991. Оп.3. Д.1. Л.12–13**;
- published as machinewritten copy / documentary publication in M. I. Odintsov's wartime religion collection.

Public documentary text:
- `https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Istorija_Tserkvi/istorija-gosudarstvenno-konfessionalnyh-otnoshenij-v-rossii-20-nachalo-21-veka-vypusk-2/2`

### What the memo closes

The memo explicitly states:

1. **M. A. Orlov**, as chairman of the All-Union Council of Evangelical Christians and Baptists, approached SDRK asking permission to convene a Moscow meeting of leading Evangelical Christians and Baptists for organizational formalization of the All-Union Council.
2. SDRK **supported** the petition.
3. Polyansky described the 1942 combined centre as having arisen `явочным порядком, без надлежащего организационного оформления`.
4. He identified the united centre's wartime patriotic appeals and foreign contacts as politically relevant.
5. He reported dissatisfaction among some Baptist representatives, especially in Ukraine, with the informally organized centre.
6. He considered formalization `настоятельно необходимым`.
7. He referred back to the 1920 unity attempt as historical precedent.
8. The proposed July plan envisioned personally inviting only **25–30** people, while the final October meeting had 45 voting delegates.

### Causality verdict

This document rejects a false binary.

It proves **church initiative at the petition stage**:
> Orlov requests the meeting.

It simultaneously proves **active state-policy selection/support**:
> Polyansky endorses the request and gives state reasons for formalizing the centre.

Safe narrator wording:
> `Орлов ходатайствовал о совещании; Совет по делам религиозных культов поддержал ходатайство, поскольку государство также считало оформление единого центра политически и административно целесообразным.`

Reject:
> `Полянский придумал объединение и приказал Орлову провести съезд.`

Reject:
> `Государство лишь пассивно зарегистрировало уже полностью сложившееся независимое объединение.`

Status: **DUAL-INITIATIVE / PERMISSION MECHANISM CLOSED**.

# PART II — 1942: joint work before formal Union

## 4. May 1942 — Baptist approach to VSEKh

M. A. Orlov's published 1944 report says:
- in **May 1942** Baptist leaders **M. I. Golyaev** and **N. A. Levindanto** approached VSEKh as the legally existing Evangelical Christian union organ;
- they proposed that VSEKh take care of / supervise Baptist congregations as it did Evangelical Christian congregations;
- the proposal was accepted;
- joint VSEKhIB work began;
- at the end of May the combined council issued an international anti-fascist Christian appeal.

Brotherly Herald transcript control:
- meeting record, Orlov report.

Independent later sources repeat the 1942 transition:
- serious denominational history;
- Orthodox Encyclopedia;
- scholarship on Soviet Protestant wartime policy.

### Claim calibration

Strong:
> `Совместная работа центра ЕХ и баптистских представителей началась в 1942 году.`

Strong to published church-source level:
> `Орлов представлял майскую инициативу как обращение Голяева и Левинданто к легально действовавшему ВСЕХ.`

Needs state-side documentary upgrade:
> exact permission/authorization sequence by which particular leaders were permitted to travel / represent communities in 1942.

Status: **1942 JOINT-WORK EVENT STRONG / EXACT STATE AUTHORIZATION CHAIN OPEN**.

## 5. Do not prematurely call the 1942 body a fully constituted national Union

Polyansky's 24 Jul 1944 memo calls the combined centre created in 1942:
- functioning;
- but `явочным` and **not duly organizationally formalized**.

Therefore distinguish:

- **1942** — combined / provisional working centre, joint leadership practice;
- **Oct 1944** — formally adopted one Union + governing All-Union Council through the permitted all-Union meeting.

Avoid:
> `ВСЕХБ был юридически учрежден в мае 1942.`

And avoid the opposite:
> `до октября 1944 никакого совместного органа не существовало.`

# PART III — war as causal environment, not a magic switch

## 7. The war changed both church life and state incentives

Brotherly Herald's 1944 proceedings repeatedly place joint work inside wartime experience:
- patriotic appeals;
- believers serving in the Red Army and wartime labor;
- women's practical aid;
- evacuation of VSEKh leadership to Ulyanovsk as front approached Moscow;
- correspondence with churches in Allied countries;
- visitation of congregations as occupied territory was liberated.

State-side July 1944 memo identifies:
- patriotic activity of the combined council;
- international contacts with US/UK religious leaders;
- need for an administratively usable centre on newly liberated territory.

### Correct analytical formulation

War was **not**:
> an automatic grant of religious freedom.

War was:
> a context in which the Soviet state's incentives toward selected religious institutions changed, while religious leaders also used the available legal space to reconnect congregations and rebuild coordination.

This distinction is essential because Chapter 17 will show that legal space remained controlled and conditional.

---

## 8. Foreign contacts are a real source line, not decorative context

Brotherly Herald records messages/contacts with:
- James Henry Rushbrooke / Baptist World Alliance;
- Walter Lewis / US Baptist leadership;
- other foreign evangelical contacts.

Polyansky's memo explicitly notes the political/public influence of Baptist leaders in the US and UK and treats the united centre's foreign connections as relevant.

Therefore foreign ties had at least two simultaneous meanings:

1. **church meaning** — old transnational Baptist/evangelical fellowship, prayer, aid, recognition;
2. **state meaning** — potentially useful external public/political channel during the Allied war relationship.

Do not collapse them into:
> `foreign Baptists created the Union`

or:
> `international contacts were irrelevant to Soviet policy`.

# PART IV — May–October 1944 state architecture

## 9. SDRK — exact date correction

Canonical chronology:

- **19 May 1944** — SNK USSR Decree No.572 creates SDRK;
- **29 May 1944** — subsequent regulation/staff structure approved;
- **24 Jul 1944** — Polyansky memo supports Orlov's request for an all-Union meeting;
- **26–29 Oct 1944** — meeting takes place in Moscow.

Old Research / Orlov speech wording `July 1944 creation of SDRK` remains a quotation-level historical witness only.

> `В октябрьском докладе Орлов датировал образование Совета июлем, однако постановление СНК №572 учреждало его 19 мая 1944 года.`

This is a useful example of source criticism rather than an embarrassing discrepancy to hide.

## 10. What SDRK was

Decree No.572 defines SDRK as a government liaison body with leaders of non-Orthodox religious organizations, including `sectarian` confessions, for matters requiring government resolution.

This means the body was neither:
- a church synod;
- nor a neutral registrar detached from policy.

It was part of the Soviet administrative system governing the permitted religious field.

For Chapter 16:
> permission for the October meeting belongs inside this administrative architecture.

For Chapter 17:
> the same architecture becomes part of postwar legal/control space.

## 14. Formal decision

Published resolution states that, setting aside past disagreements, the two unions create:

> `СОЮЗ ЕВАНГЕЛЬСКИХ ХРИСТИАН И БАПТИСТОВ`

with:

> `Всесоюзный Совет евангельских христиан и баптистов`

as governing body in Moscow.

The resolution was recorded as unanimously adopted.

### Naming control

For 1944 use period-accurate:

- `ВСЕХиБ` for the governing council.

Do not silently project the later compact `ВСЕХБ` naming backward where exact 1944 document terminology matters.

## 15. `Положение` is more than organizational paperwork

The ten-point Regulation establishes:
- one Union across USSR;
- one All-Union Council;
- common naming of congregations;
- ordained presbyter/deacon preference;
- rules for who may administer baptism, communion and marriage;
- mutual recognition of baptism/marriage with or without laying on of hands;
- a recommended harmonized practice;
- communion-practice accommodation.

This is important because the 1944 union was not merely:
> `two logos joined under one office`.

The delegates also negotiated **concrete ecclesial practices** that had historically distinguished the two streams.

Narrative implication:
> state permission created the legal opportunity; delegates still had real church questions to resolve.

# PART VI — disputed causality

## 18. `The state created the Union` — too strong without qualification

Evidence against the simple version:
- 1920 unity precedent existed;
- church leaders had long-standing unity language/prayer;
- Baptist representatives approached VSEKh in 1942 in the church's own published account;
- joint work preceded SDRK;
- **Orlov** petitioned SDRK in July 1944;
- delegates actually debated and adopted ecclesial terms.

Therefore state action cannot be the entire causal story.

---

## 19. `The state merely allowed an organic union` — also too weak

Evidence against the simple version:
- 1942 centre functioned without full organizational formalization;
- formal all-Union meeting required government permission;
- Polyansky explicitly evaluated the desirability of centralization for state purposes;
- SDRK helped enable the meeting;
- the state had its own interest in a manageable single centre on liberated territory and in foreign-facing religious contacts.

Therefore state policy was constitutive to the legal/institutional form that became possible in 1944.

---

## 20. Strong synthesis

Preferred chapter formulation:

> **The 1944 Union was neither a purely police construction nor a purely autonomous church reunion. It emerged where a real, older evangelical-baptist desire for cooperation and the wartime need to restore church coordination intersected with a Soviet policy that now preferred a permitted, centralized and administratively legible religious centre.**

This is an analytical synthesis; sources for each component must remain visible.

# PART VII — release / return of leaders

## 21. `Leaders were released from camps in 1942 to create the Union` — HOLD as a blanket claim

Later sources often state that leaders previously imprisoned in the late 1930s returned / were released around 1942 and then participated in the provisional centre.

Examples frequently mentioned:
- Ya. I. Zhidkov;
- other surviving leaders.

But the blanket sentence:
> `В 1942 году государство специально освободило руководителей ЕХБ из лагерей, чтобы создать новый союз`

requires person-by-person release files and state decision evidence.

Current rule:
- each leader gets an individual repression/release timeline;
- distinguish ordinary sentence expiry, early release, administrative transfer and specifically ordered recall;
- do not infer motive from temporal proximity.

2. Orlov's wartime legal status and authorization;
3. Andreev visitation authorization;
4. Levindanto / Golyaev movement/representation authorization;
5. any NKGB/SDRK documents expressly linking release to use in religious administration.
