## 2. Why they were seen as socially invasive

### Quote B — anti-Baptist description preserved in Baptist retelling
MBChurch article reproduces a hostile characterization of Baptist methods:

> «Такое быстрое и большое распространение баптизма коренится в свойственных сектантским группировкам методах пропаганды своего учения... Методы эти характеризуются чрезвычайной агрессивностью и воинственностью...»

It goes on to stress:
- preaching in trains, factories, villages, private houses,
- outreach beyond prayer meetings,
- and the duty of every sectarian to propagate the teaching.

### Why it matters
This is gold for showing that “sect” meant not only wrong doctrine, but hyper-active lay mission.

### Best insertion targets
- “Why society feared them”
- “Mission as normal Baptist life”
- “Orthodox counter-mission vs evangelical expansion”

## 4. Liberal or public-intellectual ambivalence

### Quote D — Dostoevsky witness
A public-intellectual witness notes that the people had become especially susceptible to new influences and explicitly mentions shtundists in that atmosphere.

This is useful not as a neutral endorsement, but as evidence that the movement had entered the public imagination as part of a larger crisis of people, Church and society.

### Why it matters
Shows that the Baptist/Stundist question was not confined to clerical polemic; it lived in broader elite anxieties.

### Best insertion targets
- public opinion chapter
- introduction to “religious ferment after emancipation”

## 5. Liberal press vs anti-sect panic

### Quote E — anti-sect witness about liberal sympathy
Orthodox polemical literature itself complains that some people reacted to shtundism sympathetically and that the liberal press saw it as something “отрадное, светлое, желательное”.

### Why it matters
Even hostile witnesses preserve evidence that the reception was divided.

### Best insertion targets
- section: “Not all public opinion was anti-Baptist”
- compare Church-state conservatives vs liberals/moderates

## 6. Polunov’s framing — two incompatible readings of the same movement

### Summary quote idea
Polunov’s 2012 article is indispensable for one sentence summary:
- ecclesiastical authorities treated evangelical movements as ignorance and threat;
- educated society, liberals and moderate conservatives could view them as a reflection of post-reform change and as a response to the shortcomings of the official Church.

### Why it matters
This is one of the strongest “book-level” frames for the whole series.

### Best insertion targets
- macro-introduction to the whole project
- any chapter on public reception
- chapter on 1905 religious freedom debates

## 7. 1905 was an opening, not instant acceptance

### Quote F
Public-history and legal studies agree that the 1905-1906 changes allowed legal transition from Orthodoxy to other Christian confessions and gave Protestants, especially Baptists, more air.

One compact public formulation:
- after the 1905 decree, Russian Protestants, especially Baptists, “впервые смогли вздохнуть свободно”.

### Why it matters
It is important to show that legalization changed the rules, but did not eliminate stigma.

### Best insertion targets
- legal status chapter
- chapter on freedom of conscience
- transition from persecution to tolerated-yet-suspect existence

## 9. “The Damned Stundist” — extreme image-making

### Quote H
Coleman’s work preserves a striking example from Kharkov diocese: a brochure titled **“The Damned Stundist”**, apparently used in schools, speaking of the “outcast race of shtundists”.

Selected lines from the English rendering:
> “The simple-hearted but peep in / To the lair of the perfidious beast, / By detraction, slander, and cajolery / They are entrapped by the damned Stundist.”

### Why it matters
This is one of the strongest pieces in the whole “public image / moral panic” archive.

### Best insertion targets
- chapter on anti-sect propaganda
- box/sidebar on “how children and ordinary readers were taught to imagine shtundists”
- contrast with later Baptist self-memory

## 14. Kargel as bridge figure

### Quote M — Gregory Nichols
Kargel’s contacts with Radstock/Pashkovite evangelicalism, Brethren influence, Holiness/Keswick spirituality, and broader Evangelical Alliance instincts challenged his stricter Baptist starting point and opened wider spiritual possibilities.

### Why it matters
Kargel is not just “another preacher”; he is a hinge figure between:
- German Baptist grounding,
- Pashkovite broader evangelicalism,
- Brethren/Holiness/Keswick spirituality,
- and Russian evangelical theology.

### Best insertion targets
- Kargel bridge chapter
- section on why Russian Evangelical Christianity cannot be flattened into one imported template

## 15. Soviet late reclassification: church and sect at once

### Quote N — Panich
Panich’s core insight may be paraphrased almost verbatim:
- Soviet power could encourage a more church-like, centralized, visible structure,
- while still preserving sect stigma where convenient,
- especially for dissident wings.

### Why it matters
This is crucial for all later chapters on ВСЕХБ / Council of Churches / initiative group / underground press.

### Best insertion targets
- chapter on postwar institutionalization
- chapter on split between official and unregistered streams
- chapter on public image in late Soviet society
