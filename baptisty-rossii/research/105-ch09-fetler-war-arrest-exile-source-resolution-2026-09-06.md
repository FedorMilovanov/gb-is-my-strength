## 0. Why this resolution exists

Late Russian-language biographies often compress Fetler's wartime removal into one dramatic sentence:

> arrested in late 1914 → sentenced to Siberia/Yakutia → sentence commuted to permanent/lifetime exile abroad.

That formula mixes:
- old/new calendar dates;
- later memory;
- legal terminology;
- geographic variants (`Сибирь` / `Якутия`);
- and a `пожизненное` claim that requires stronger proof.

Book Authority requires the sequence to be decomposed instead of copied.

## 1. Near-contemporary 1915 witness — strongest new control

### Object

*The Missionary Review of the World*, April 1915, p. 311, World-Wide Missionary News, item **`Pastor Fetler in Exile`**.

Public PDF witness:
`https://cafis.org/files/MRW-1915-4.pdf`

Annual index independently lists:
- `Fetler in Exile, Pastor` — p. **311**;
- `Fetler's New Work, Pastor` — p. 542.

### What the item reports

The 1915 item says, in substance:
- Fetler was already writing from **Stockholm**, where he was in exile;
- after the war began, Baptists/Evangelical Christians were attacked in the press as German/German-funded;
- Fetler was arrested and brought to prison, intended for transport to Siberia;
- the sentence/outcome was reduced to **exile from Russia during the entire war**;
- it also reports subsequent banishments/closures affecting other Protestant workers.

### Evidence classification

This is not a Russian court order and is not neutral state documentation.

But chronologically it is much closer to the event than later biographies and directly contradicts the unqualified `lifetime exile` formula.

## 3. Calendar resolution: November vs December 1914

Late biography formula:
> `последняя суббота, 22 ноября 1914 года`.

Calendar test:
- 22 Nov 1914 Gregorian = Sunday;
- 22 Nov 1914 Julian = 5 Dec 1914 Gregorian;
- 5 Dec 1914 Gregorian = Saturday.

This strongly indicates the `22 Nov` tradition is **Old Style**.

Therefore:
- `22 Nov 1914 O.S.`
- `5 Dec 1914 N.S.`

This substantially reconciles sources saying `November 1914` with those summarizing `December 1914`.

See file 104 for the full calendar contract.

State: **APPARENT MONTH CONFLICT SUBSTANTIALLY RESOLVED**.

## 5. `Lifetime exile` resolution

Evidence currently available:

### Supports limited/wartime exile
- *Missionary Review*, Apr 1915, p. 311: exile from Russia **during the entire war**.

### Later sources use stronger wording
- several late church-history/biographical sources say `пожизненное изгнание` / permanent expulsion.

### Canonical Chapter 9 rule

Until a primary Russian order or comparably strong legal source is recovered:

- **DO NOT use `пожизненное изгнание` as unqualified fact**;
- prefer `высылка/изгнание из России`;
- where duration matters, attribute the near-contemporary 1915 formulation `на время войны`;
- mention the stronger lifetime/permanent version only as a later-source discrepancy if necessary.

State: **`LIFETIME` CLAIM DOWNGRADED TO HOLD**.

## 6. Wartime motive / anti-German suspicion

Multiple source layers support a broad wartime context in which Protestant/Evangelical groups faced suspicion of German sympathy/support.

The 1915 Missionary Review item explicitly reports such press accusations before describing Fetler's arrest.

Fetler's 1917 article reproduces/answers accusations casting him as German or a German political agent.

Academic/secondary history likewise describes wartime anti-Protestant pressure and deportations.

### Safe synthesis

> `Первая мировая война сделала международные связи русских протестантов политически подозрительными; Фетлер оказался одной из наиболее заметных целей этой военной подозрительности.`

This is a synthesis and should be supported by more than Fetler's own voice.

### Do not overclaim

Do not say:
- `Фетлера выслали только потому, что он был латышом`;
- `государство доказало немецкое финансирование`;
- `обвинение в германофильстве было единственным юридическим основанием`;
without legal files.
