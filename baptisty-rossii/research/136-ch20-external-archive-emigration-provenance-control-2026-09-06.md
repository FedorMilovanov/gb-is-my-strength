## 0. Why this file exists

Chapter 20 uses external archives as historical evidence.

That creates a specific danger:

> a document arrives in a Western archive, receives an English title, a box/folder locator and a clean catalog record — and the later researcher unconsciously treats that archival description as if it were the object's original Soviet identity and transmission history.

That is not safe.

A cross-border object has multiple layers:

```text
original Soviet creator/event
       ↓
first physical/textual object
       ↓
copy / retyping / samizdat reproduction
       ↓
carrier / intermediary / border crossing
       ↓
foreign recipient
       ↓
verification / translation / excerpting
       ↓
publication / advocacy / broadcast
       ↓
archive accession
       ↓
archivist arrangement + catalog metadata
       ↓
digitization / modern web record
```

Every arrow can change what survives and how it is described.

# A. KESTON AS AN ACTIVE INFORMATION INSTITUTION

## 1. Keston was not merely a passive box repository

Baylor's institutional histories explicitly state that Keston:
- began in 1969 as the Center for the Study of Religion and Communism;
- collected information from believers behind the Iron Curtain;
- **verified** it;
- **translated** it;
- **disseminated** it;
- created publications, research files and Keston News Service output;
- later transferred the Archive and Library to Baylor in 2007.

Authoritative controls:
- Michael Bourdeaux Papers, KC.24:
  `https://libraryarchives.baylor.edu/repositories/4/resources/472`
- Keston Institute Records:
  `https://libraryarchives.baylor.edu/repositories/4/resources/148`
- Baylor Keston About:
  `https://library.web.baylor.edu/visit/keston-center-religion-politics-society/about`

### Editorial consequence

Do not describe a Keston copy as simply:
> `советский документ из архива Keston`.

Where possible distinguish:
- Soviet original/copy;
- Keston working copy;
- Keston translation;
- Keston report based on received material;
- later Baylor digital surrogate.

---

## 2. `Verified by Keston` is not the same as `archivally proven today`

Keston's institutional practice of checking information is historically valuable.

But for this book:
- a Keston report is an external contemporary/near-contemporary source;
- it does not automatically replace the underlying Soviet document;
- its verification method may not be recoverable for every claim;
- the original informant may have been anonymous for safety;
- repeated Western reports may all derive from one Soviet samizdat object.

Production rule:
> count source independence by **document lineage**, not by number of publications that repeated it.

---

## 5. 32 boxes vs 6 boxes is NOT a historical statistic

Reject every inference of this type:

> `Because KC.29 has 32 boxes and KC.22 only 6, the unregistered movement was five times larger / more persecuted / more important.`

Archive extent reflects:
- collection priorities;
- survival;
- donor/network access;
- density of samizdat production;
- duplication;
- later acquisitions;
- archival processing choices;
- different date spans and genres.

The size difference is a property of the **archive**, not a census of Soviet Baptists.

# C. ARRANGEMENT CONTROL

## 6. Box/folder locator ≠ original Soviet filing order

Both KC.22 and KC.29 descriptions say:
- Keston received many materials already grouped by categories and preserved much of that filing system;
- items received with no particular order were arranged thematically and chronologically.

Therefore an archival sequence such as:

```text
Box 7 / Folder 2
Box 7 / Folder 3
Box 7 / Folder 6
```

proves current archival adjacency/order.

It does **not** by itself prove:
- the documents travelled together;
- one was attached to another;
- Soviet creators filed them together;
- they reached Keston in that order;
- the box/folder sequence reflects chronology of transmission.

### Required language

Use:
> `в нынешнем архивном описании Keston/Baylor документ находится...`

Not:
> `документ был передан вместе с...`

unless accession/custodial records prove it.

# D. VINS — A MODEL OF MULTIPLE ARCHIVAL LIVES

## 7. Centre for Mennonite Brethren Studies Vins collection

Collection:
- `Georgi Vins (Wiens) collection`;
- dates: 1979, 1984–1986, 1989, 1998;
- physical extent: 3 cm textual records;
- Volume 1092.

Public finding aid:
- `https://cmbs.mennonitebrethren.ca/personal_papers/vins-wiensgeorgi-1928-1998/`

File 1 includes:
- Vins CSCE testimony, 7 Jun 1979;
- International Representation publications, May and Nov 1984;
- 1984 prisoner directory;
- later memorial material.

File 2 includes:
- Vins testimony / related material, 1979–1983.

---

## 8. Its custodial history is explicitly unclear

The finding aid says:
- history of the material is unclear;
- material appears to have been gathered from various sources and assembled into the collection;
- some second-file material came from Ewald Unruh;
- some material was gathered after Vins's death.

This is a high-value methodological example.

### What the collection proves well

It can strongly prove:
- that these documentary objects/publications are held together in a later Vins collection;
- bibliographic identity of listed 1979/1984 items;
- that Vins testimony was circulated as a publication/object;
- that International Representation was producing named publications by 1984.

### What it does not prove by itself

It cannot automatically prove:
- who first possessed each object;
- exact chain from Vins to archive;
- whether listed copies are originals, first-generation copies or later reproductions;
- which objects Vins personally deposited;
- exact founding date of International Representation.

Production rule:
> never use the fonds title `Georgi Vins collection` as shorthand for `Vins personally preserved/donated this document`.

# G. ORIGINAL LANGUAGE / TRANSLATION

## 11. Always preserve original-language identity

Keston collections contain multiple languages, including Russian and English.

A file may contain:
- Russian original;
- Russian carbon/typed copy;
- Keston English translation;
- English summary;
- Western publication excerpt;
- translated quotation reprinted elsewhere.

For high-value quotes record fields:

```text
original_language
original_object_status
translation_creator
translation_date
translation_status
translation_source_object
publication/reprint
```

Do not quote a later English Keston rendering and silently translate it back into Russian as if recovering the original wording.

That produces a `Russian → English → modern Russian` round-trip distortion.

---

## 12. Back-translation ban

If original Russian survives:
- quote Russian original;
- use Keston translation only as an independent transmission witness.

If only English translation is currently accessible:
- paraphrase carefully;
- mark `ORIGINAL-LANGUAGE OBJECT OPEN`;
- do not fabricate a quotation in Russian quotation marks.

This rule is especially important for:
- prisoner appeals;
- church-state accusations;
- theological/ecclesial wording;
- legal testimony.

# H. DUPLICATE / DERIVATIVE CONTROL

## 13. One Soviet object can create many Western records

Example pattern:

```text
Russian appeal
 → Keston copy
 → English translation
 → Keston News Service item
 → parliamentary briefing
 → newspaper story
 → church newsletter
 → later book
```

That is **one originating testimony with multiple reception witnesses**, not seven independent witnesses to the underlying event.

Evidence ledger must distinguish:
- origin independence;
- reception independence.

Both are useful, but for different claims.

### Origin claim
`What happened in the USSR?`
- need original/internal/state corroboration.

### Reception claim
`How widely was this event known abroad?`
- multiple independent Western receptions are legitimate evidence.

# I. SILENCE / ABSENCE CONTROL

## 14. Not in Keston ≠ did not happen

Absence from a finding aid cannot prove absence in Soviet church life.

Possible reasons:
- document never crossed border;
- sender destroyed it;
- recipient did not retain it;
- still unprocessed;
- filed under another denomination/topic/person;
- described at a higher level;
- privacy/donor restrictions;
- acquisition gaps.

Production ban:
> do not argue from `I did not find it in Keston` to `the event/position did not exist`.

---

# J. EXTERNAL ADVOCACY ≠ INTERNAL REPRESENTATIVENESS

## 15. Vins's international prominence must not become a denominator

After 1979 Vins had unusual access to:
- press;
- churches;
- U.S. official forums;
- international travel;
- advocacy infrastructure.

That makes him an exceptionally important witness.

It does **not** prove:
- every unregistered Baptist agreed with every statement;
- he represented all Soviet Baptists;
- registered Baptist voices were absent;
- his later framing maps perfectly onto every region/time in the USSR.

Production wording:
> `Винс стал одним из наиболее заметных международных представителей незарегистрированного баптистского движения.`

Avoid:
> `Винс говорил от имени советских баптистов` without exact mandate/scope.

---

# K. EXILE / CITIZENSHIP CONTROL

## 16. `Emigrant`, `exile`, `expelled`, `stateless/citizenship-deprived` are not synonyms

The Mennonite Brethren finding aid says Vins was stripped of Soviet citizenship and exiled in the 27 Apr 1979 exchange; it also states the exile was lifted in 1990.

These are strong biographical archive-level claims.

But Book-ready legal language still needs:
- Soviet citizenship-deprivation decree/instrument;
- exact authority/date;
- 1990 restoration/lifting instrument;
- citizenship status after that act.

Until then:
- `выслан/оказался в США в результате обмена` is safe to official U.S. level;
- `лишен гражданства` may be attributed to strong biographical/archival sources;
- exact legal formula remains P0.

Do not call this simply `эмиграцией` where agency matters.

# L. ARCHIVE RIGHTS / PUBLICATION

## 17. Research access ≠ publication permission

Baylor explicitly states:
- Keston collections are available for research subject to collection restrictions;
- copying requests require a formal process;
- Keston/Baylor may not own copyright in deposited materials;
- user must determine copyright holder and publication rights;
- permission to make a research copy does not itself grant publication permission;
- attribution should identify Keston Center/Baylor where applicable.

Controls:
- Keston Usage Guidelines:
  `https://library.web.baylor.edu/visit/keston-center-religion-politics-society/research/usage-guidelines`
- Keston Citation Guide:
  `https://library.web.baylor.edu/visit/keston-center-religion-politics-society/research/citation-guide`
- Baylor digital collections policy:
  `https://library.web.baylor.edu/about/libraries-policies`

### Production consequence

`publicly viewable Baylor scan` = **ACCESS**, not automatic `RIGHTS_CLEARED`.

Every final image needs:
- object copyright assessment;
- scan/digital surrogate terms;
- requested attribution;
- publication permission/fair-use determination as applicable.

# N. THREE TYPES OF PROVENANCE

## 20. Separate them

### P1 — historical provenance
Who created the original object, when, and for whom?

### P2 — transmission provenance
How did it move from the USSR to the foreign recipient/public sphere?

### P3 — archival provenance
How did the current archive acquire, arrange, describe and digitize the surviving object?

A strong Chapter 20 object may have:
- P1 closed;
- P2 partly open;
- P3 closed.

Do not collapse that to `provenance verified`.

# P. SOURCE-INDEPENDENCE MATRIX

## 23. How to count voices

| Pair | Independent for underlying Soviet event? | Independent for reception/history of circulation? |
|---|---|---|
| Russian original + Keston English translation | NO | YES, for translation history |
| Russian appeal + Keston News Service report based solely on it | usually NO | YES |
| Russian appeal + Soviet court file | potentially YES | YES |
| Vins testimony + newspaper report of same testimony | NO for claims sourced only to Vins | YES for public reception |
| Vins testimony + registered Baptist rebuttal | YES as competing participant voices | YES |
| KC.29 copy + MB Studies copy of same publication | NO for content | YES for archival circulation |
| church samizdat + U.S. diplomatic cable independently reporting official Soviet discussion | potentially YES | YES |

This distinction should control footnote/source counting.

# 25. Current provenance verdict

The external archive is not merely a warehouse after the story.

It is part of the story itself:

> **believers produced documents under pressure; some crossed a controlled border; foreign institutions translated and tested them; governments, churches and media reacted; archives then rearranged and preserved the resulting paper trail; digitization has now returned parts of that trail to readers who can compare it with Soviet and church records.**

The methodological rule is equally important:

> **the cleaner the modern catalog record looks, the more carefully the book must distinguish original object, transmission, translation, archival arrangement and publication rights.**
