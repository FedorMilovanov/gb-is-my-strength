Emigration to America + Central Asia. Verified 2026-06-19. Reader text per editorial policy.

--- GAMEO "Migrations" ---
"In 1874-80 some 18,000 Mennonites left Russia to settle in the United States and Canada... The chief reasons for this mass migration were unwillingness to accept a compulsory alternative service program and the objection to a Russianization program... many were also responding to an acute land shortage."

--- GAMEO "Mennonite Brethren Church" ---
"When the government of Russia instituted universal military service in 1870, the Mennonites, who had thus far enjoyed complete exemption... were in danger of losing their privileges. The government had decreed that Mennonite settlers should be under obligation to render such service beginning with 1880. Though this order was later modified so that Mennonite young men could substitute work in the forestry department instead, many decided to emigrate to North America. In 1874 this emigration to the New World set in... settling mainly in Kansas, Nebraska, Minnesota, and North and South Dakota. No Mennonite Brethren members settled in Canada at this time."
"From 1923 to 1929 many of the Mennonite Brethren migrated to Canada; others later found a new home in South America. We can hardly speak of a functioning MB Church in Russia since 1929."

--- Wikipedia "Russian Mennonites" ---
"Between 1874 and 1880, of the approximately 45,000 Mennonites in South Russia, 10,000 departed for the United States and 8,000 for Manitoba... For those who remained in Russia, the military service question was resolved by 1880 with a substitute four-year forestry service program for men of military age."
"Others looked east, and in one of the strangest chapters of Mennonite history, Claas Epp, Jr., Abraham Peters and other leaders led hundreds of Mennonites to Central Asia in the 1880s, where they expected Christ's imminent return. They settled in the Talas Valley of Turkestan and in the Khanate of Khiva."

--- Anabaptist World / mhsc.ca / Bethel ML ---
"Between 1874 and the mid-1880s, about a third of the Mennonites in the Russian Empire and Prussia migrated to the North American Midwest. About 10,000 settled in the United States, 8,000 in Canada." Delegation of 12 (1873), incl. Elder Jacob Buller (Alexanderwohl). 1 Jan 1874 law: mandatory service with noncombatant (forestry/industrial peacetime, hospital wartime).

--- US State Dept FRUS 1872 (history.state.gov/.../frus1872p1/d375) ---
Mennonites (~150k souls across ~100 colonies) propose to seek new homes in America to avoid military service; came to Russia from Prussia in 1789 on invitation of Catherine II with promise of "exemption forever from military service."

EDITORIAL: This is primarily MENNONITE emigration. Russian Baptists/Evangelical Christians did NOT emigrate en masse in 1874 - separate from the Tiflis/southern Baptist lines. Later waves (post-1917, 1920s MB to Canada/S.America; 1970s ECB/Pentecostals to US) are distinct.
