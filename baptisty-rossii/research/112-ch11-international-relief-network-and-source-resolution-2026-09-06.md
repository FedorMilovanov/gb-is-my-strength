## 0. Purpose

This file prevents three recurrent errors:
1. treating all American relief as one organization;
2. treating all money as one accounting stream;
3. treating relief, Bible funds, mission funds and unity negotiations as interchangeable.

The evidence supports a network model, not one pipeline.

Core actors:

## 2. Network B — Northern Baptists / ABFMS

Core actors/institutions:
- American Baptist Foreign Mission Society;
- W. O. Lewis;
- J. H. Rushbrooke as European Baptist coordinator;
- ARA / Herbert Hoover transport and distribution infrastructure;
- Russian Baptist and Evangelical Christian leaders/local centers.

Contemporary ABFMS Annual Report 1923 establishes:
- fall 1921 `Ship of Fellowship` relief as predecessor;
- renewed 1922 campaign `Rush a Ship to Russia`;
- nearly 7,000 packages in the second campaign;
- 1,091 bales clothing + 8 children's-clothing bales + 26 cases miscellaneous supplies;
- ARA ocean transport without denomination freight cost;
- Odessa unloading/inspection;
- Lewis + Rushbrooke conference in Moscow;
- creation of an all-Russian Baptist-Evangelical relief committee;
- multi-center distribution;
- substantial ARA-directed distribution irrespective of recipients' religious affiliation.

Rule:
> These shipment quantities describe one specific Northern Baptist relief operation. They are not the total value/volume of all Baptist relief to Russia.

## 3. Network C — Southern Baptists

Core actor:
- Everett Gill.

Contemporary Southern Baptist sources establish renewed famine-era contact with Russian Baptists and Hoover/relief access. A March 1922 US newspaper page reproduces Gill with P. V. Pavlov, supporting direct contact in the same period.

Potapova's primary-source reconstruction from `Сеятель истины` adds:
- Southern Baptist appeal to churches for clothing/items for suffering believers in Russia/Ukraine;
- Gill in Moscow and traveling in Russia;
- consultation with Russian Baptist leaders;
- distributed local relief points rather than one central distribution location.

Separation rule:
- **physical famine relief** = Chapter 11 core;
- **$7,000 Southern Baptist + $7,000 ABFMS Bible/New Testament appropriations** = Bible/mission funding, mostly Chapter 12 bridge;
- do not label the $14,000 Bible project as famine-relief cash.

## 5. Network E — Baptist World Alliance / European coordination

Institutional background:
- 1919 Rushbrooke + C. A. Brooks assess postwar European needs;
- 1920 London Baptist conference creates coordinated help/relief framework;
- Rushbrooke becomes BWA Europe Commissioner.

By Russia famine period this network performs:
- information gathering;
- coordination among British/North American/European Baptist bodies;
- relief oversight;
- Russian visits;
- connection between relief and later questions of mission/education/representation.

BWA Resolution 1923.3 at Stockholm is the official public theological/institutional summary of this relief ethos.

## 6. ARA role — exact scope

ARA is not a Baptist organization and must never be described as one.

Closed functions in the current evidence:
- food-draft / warehouse mechanism used or recommended by Russian-speaking Baptist diaspora sources;
- ocean transport for a Northern Baptist relief shipment;
- distribution cooperation in Russia;
- some distribution explicitly irrespective of religious affiliation.

Open:
- exact ARA records corresponding to individual Baptist food drafts and named local Baptist recipients;
- exact contractual terms for each Baptist organization;
- full list of Baptist/Evangelical distribution centers.

Narrative rule:
> ARA supplied logistics and large-scale relief infrastructure into which religious relief networks could plug; Baptist/Evangelical organizations supplied money, goods, appeals, local knowledge, trusted contacts and distribution structures.

## 7. Relief ≠ evangelism, but the channels overlapped

The same people and organizations often handled:
- famine relief;
- Bibles/New Testaments;
- missionary support;
- theological school plans;
- international representation.

This creates historical overlap, not accounting identity.

Book rule:
- label each fund/object by purpose;
- distinguish relief cargo from Bible cargo;
- distinguish emergency aid from recurring mission support;
- distinguish local relief committee from union governance;
- avoid teleology: relief did not automatically produce later institutional projects.

---

## 8. Relief ≠ union, but aid exposed governance problems

ABFMS 1923 records both:
- relief cooperation in Russia;
- explicit foreign Baptist support for Russian Baptist/Evangelical Christian union.

Archive-based Disciples material indicates some grants/administrative plans increasingly sought representative joint structures.

Safe synthesis:
> The famine did not cause the merger question, which predated it. But relief made divided representation practically expensive: foreign partners wanted to know who could receive, account for and distribute help across the two related Russian movements.

Forbidden:
- `the West forced the merger`;
- `aid was payment for union`;
- `all foreign agencies demanded one organization`.
