# 117. Chapter 12 — joint → separate Bible courses, 1923–1928: source resolution

Дата прохода: 2026-09-06  
Authority: `docs/BAPTISTY-ROSSII-BOOK-AUTHORITY-V2.md`  
Статус: **CHRONOLOGY RESOLVED AT NARRATIVE LEVEL / PRIMARY ROSTERS PARTIAL**

## 0. Question

Did Baptists and Evangelical Christians create one lasting Bible school after Stockholm 1923?

Current answer:
> **No.** The evidence supports a real joint educational experiment in Leningrad in 1924, followed by separate denominational trajectories rather than one durable common school.

---

## 1. 1923 — College Fund is still an international institutional question

BWA Resolution 1923.6, Stockholm 21–27 Jul 1923:
- Russian delegates welcomed;
- conditions judged more favorable for the original College Fund purpose;
- Dr Cree proposals referred to Executive.

This establishes foreign institutional interest, not the final structure of a Russian school.

Status: **CLOSED**.

---

## 2. 1924 — joint experiment is real

Evidence stack:

### Walter Oliver Lewis Collection finding aid
- 3 Apr 1924: Committee of All-Russia's Baptist Union — report on Bible courses in Leningrad.
- 1924 Rushbrooke correspondence on Russian mission questions.

### MRW 1925
Indexed contemporary page:
- school image labeled Leningrad Bible School of Evangelical Christians;
- Prokhanov named principal;
- term 1 Jan–1 Jul 1924;
- 37 pupils reported.

### later Baptist internal memory
`Братский Вестник` №3, 1957 explicitly says one-year joint EC/Baptist preacher courses were conducted in Leningrad in 1924, then each union conducted courses separately.

Synthesis:
> The 1924 course was a genuine inter-union educational collaboration hosted inside the Leningrad Evangelical Christian setting.

Do not call it:
- a merged union seminary;
- a permanent common institution;
- exclusively Baptist;
- exclusively Evangelical Christian if discussing student participation.

---

## 3. 1925 — separation becomes documentary, but exact cohort composition needs work

Lewis finding aid:
- 1 Jan 1925: Educational Commission of All Russian Baptist Union to Collegium re Bible Courses at Leningrad Evangelical Church;
- 3 Jan 1925: Rushbrooke, Everett Gill, W. O. Lewis recommendation.

These titles show the educational relationship was still an active administrative issue after the 1924 course.

Later internal chronology says separate Evangelical Christian annual courses continued from 1925.

MASTER conflict:
- rows 967/2355 describe January 1925 Leningrad EC course yet say 50 brothers `поровну от каждого Союза`.

This may represent:
- a transitional joint intake;
- a caption conflation of 1924 and 1925;
- a later source using `1925` for an academic cycle begun earlier.

Do not resolve without primary roster/program.

Status: **SEPARATION DIRECTION STRONG / 1925 COHORT COMPOSITION HOLD**.

---

## 4. Baptist independent school was delayed, not immediate

MASTER row 2835 records:
- from 24 Jun 1924 P. V. Ivanov-Klyshnikov initiated/pursued Baptist petitions for a Bible school;
- independent Moscow Bible Courses opened only 1 Dec 1927.

MASTER direct rows 365/1441/2222 and external ECB reference agree on the 1 Dec 1927 opening.

Therefore reject:
> `After the joint course, Baptists simply opened their own school in 1925/1926.`

Better:
> Baptists pursued an independent school through several years of petitioning, organization and premises search; the full Moscow course opened in December 1927.

---

## 5. Moscow 1927 course — current controlled claims

Strongly supported:
- opening: **1 Dec 1927**;
- intended three-year program;
- first intake reported as **50**;
- MASTER reports six women;
- P. V. Ivanov-Klyshnikov as head;
- named teachers in MASTER include V. I. Sinitsyn, P. V. Ivanov-Klyshnikov, I. Ya. Miller, Ya. I. Vyazovsky, N. V. Odintsov, P. Ya. Datsko;
- closed in 1929 before the program could finish.

Primary gates:
- opening order/permission;
- full roster;
- curriculum document;
- funding/premises contract;
- exact closure order/date.

Use `50` as reported and strong until roster is received.

## 7. Why this matters to the chapter's unity thesis

The strongest evidence of failed unity is not a polemical quotation. It is the institutional sequence:

1. international College Fund question;
2. common 1924 classroom;
3. unresolved administration/representation;
4. separate denominational educational tracks;
5. Baptist independent school delayed until 1927.

Safe synthesis:
> doctrinal proximity and practical cooperation were sufficient for a shared course, but insufficient for one durable educational authority.

This avoids personalizing the failure as simply `Prokhanov versus Baptists`.
