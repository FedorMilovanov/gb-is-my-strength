## 1. Strongest Archaeological Anchors (2021–2026 discoveries)

### 1.1 The Acra of Jerusalem (City of David)
- **Discovery:** IAA Givati Parking Lot excavations (2007–2015+).
- **Finds:** Substantial remains of a Hellenistic fortress: stone walls 4m wide, defensive towers, and a sloped glacis.
- **Evidence:** Stamped Seleucid ballista stones, lead sling bullets, and bronze arrowheads.
- **Significance:** Physical proof of the Seleucid stronghold described in 1 Maccabees 1:33, which controlled Jerusalem for 25 years.

### 1.2 Bet Zechariah Battlefield (Fifth Battle)
- **Discovery:** Dvir Raviv (Bar-Ilan University), 2022 survey, published 2025/2026.
- **Site:** Horbat Bet Zecharia (Gush Etzion), 7km SW of Bethlehem.
- **Artifacts:** Cast lead sling bullets (one with the winged thunderbolt of Zeus) and a bronze coin from the city of Side (a known Seleucid mercenary recruitment center).
- **Biblical Link:** Matches the account in 1 Maccabees 6:32-47 where Judah fought Seleucid war elephants and his brother Eleazar was crushed.

### 1.3 Judean Desert Refuge (Cave of the Box)
- **Discovery:** IAA Survey, 2022.
- **Artifact:** A small wooden box containing 15 silver tetradrachms minted under Antiochus IV Epiphanes (latest coin 170 BC).
- **Significance:** Tangible evidence of Jewish flight to the wilderness described in 1 Maccabees 2:29-37 ("many... went down to hiding places in the wilderness").

### 1.4 Hasmonean Fortified Line (Lachish Forest)
- **Discovery:** IAA excavations, 2021.
- **Site:** Hellenistic garrison fort near Beit Guvrin.
- **Evidence:** 0.5m thick destruction layer, iron spearheads, sling stones, and 30+ Seleucid coins.
- **Conquest:** Likely razed by John Hyrcanus I circa 112 BC during the campaign to conquer Idumea.
