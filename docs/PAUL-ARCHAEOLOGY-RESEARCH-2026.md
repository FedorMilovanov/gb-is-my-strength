## 1. Strongest Archaeological Corroborations (2025–2026)

### 1.1 Gallio Inscription (Delphi, Greece)
- **Date:** AD 51–52
- **Significance:** Fixes Paul’s time in Corinth during Gallio’s proconsulship (Acts 18:12–17).
- **Status:** One of the strongest external anchors for Pauline chronology.

### 1.2 Erastus Inscription (Corinth)
- **Reference:** Romans 16:23 — “Erastus, the city treasurer”
- **Discovery:** 1929, confirmed multiple times. Inscription mentions “Erastus, aedile” (city official).
- **Status:** Direct match with Paul’s letter.

### 1.3 Sergius Paulus Inscription (Pisidian Antioch)
- **Reference:** Acts 13:7 — proconsul on Cyprus
- **Discovery:** Bilingual Greek/Latin inscription in Antioch of Pisidia naming Sergius Paulus.
- **Implication:** Strong evidence that the family had roots in the region Paul later visited.

### 1.4 Ephesus
- **Theater:** Seats 25,000 — matches Acts 19:29–34.
- **Tyrannus Hall:** Large lecture hall suitable for daily teaching (Acts 19:9).
- **Artemis Temple:** One of the Seven Wonders — context for the riot.

### 1.5 Philippi
- **First European church.** Excavations revealed basilica and early Christian inscriptions.
- **Philippians 16:12** — “leading city of the district” — confirmed archaeologically.

### 1.6 Athens — Areopagus
- **Mars Hill** still identifiable. Paul’s speech (Acts 17) fits the philosophical context of 1st-century Athens.
